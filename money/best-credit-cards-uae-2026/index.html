Best Credit Cards in UAE 2026: Cashback, Miles, and No-Fee Picks Compared

Walk into any UAE mall and you'll see the pitch: "Sign up today and get a free iPad!" The bank reps are aggressive, the sign-up bonuses are real, and most expats end up with a credit card they chose for the wrong reason — a freebie they'll forget in three months — while paying AED 1,000+ in annual fees for benefits they never use.

The right credit card in the UAE can put AED 4,000-8,000 back in your pocket every year through cashback, miles, and lounge access. The wrong one costs you more in fees than you earn in rewards. The difference comes down to matching the card to your actual spending pattern — not the sign-up bonus.

After comparing 20+ UAE credit cards across 6 reward categories, here are the ones that actually deliver value in 2026.

Important: This is an informational comparison, not financial advice. Credit card terms change frequently. Always verify current rates, fees, and eligibility on the bank's official website before applying. You must meet the bank's minimum income requirement and provide the required documents (salary certificate, Emirates ID, passport copy, visa).

How I Evaluated

Every card below was assessed on:

  • Reward rate: Cashback percentage or miles earning rate across common spend categories (groceries, fuel, dining, online, utilities).
  • Annual fee: What you pay per year, and whether the rewards offset it.
  • Minimum income requirement: The salary you need to qualify (ranges from AED 5,000 to AED 40,000+).
  • Sign-up bonus: Welcome offers, free tickets, bonus miles — and whether they're genuinely valuable.
  • Lounge access: Airport lounge visits per year (important for frequent travelers from DXB/AUH).
  • Foreign transaction fees: What you pay when using the card outside the UAE (2-3% is typical, some cards waive it).
  • Supplementary cards: Free additional cards for family members.
  • Digital experience: App quality, statement clarity, reward redemption ease.

I cross-referenced bank websites, UAE Central Bank disclosures, and user reviews on Google Play / App Store for each bank's app. Reward calculations are based on a typical expat monthly spend of AED 8,000-15,000 across categories.

UAE Credit Card Types: Quick Primer

Before picking a card, understand the three main types available in the UAE:

Cashback Cards

You earn a percentage of your spend back as cash, credited to your card account. Simple, transparent, no redemption headaches. Best for: People who want guaranteed value without tracking points.

Miles / Travel Cards

You earn airline miles (Emirates Skywards, Etihad Guest, etc.) that can be redeemed for flights and upgrades. Miles are worth more per dirham if you redeem for business/first class — but worthless if you never fly. Best for: Frequent travelers who fly 3+ times per year.

Low-Fee / No-Fee Cards

No annual fee, modest rewards. You won't earn as much, but you also won't pay for benefits you don't use. Best for: Light spenders, first-time credit card users, or people who already have a primary card.

Islamic cards: Most UAE banks offer Sharia-compliant credit cards (no interest, profit-based structuring). The rewards and fees are typically identical to conventional cards. Look for "Islamic" or "Sharia" in the product name if this matters to you.

The reasoning, below.

1. Emirates NBD Visa Infinite — Best for Emirates Skywards Miles

Annual fee: AED 1,250 | Min income: AED 25,000/month | Network: Visa | Islamic option: Yes

If you fly Emirates 3+ times a year, this card pays for itself. You earn 4 Skywards Miles per USD spent (roughly 1.1 miles per AED), plus bonus miles on Emirates bookings, and you get 2 complimentary airport lounge visits per quarter (8/year) at Dubai International and worldwide Priority Pass lounges.

Reward breakdown:

  • Emirates & flydubai bookings: 4 miles per USD
  • All other spend: 2 miles per USD (tiered)
  • Bonus: 25,000 welcome miles on first purchase (enough for a short-haul flight)

Why it works for UAE residents: Emirates is the default airline for most Dubai-based expats. Skywards miles are easy to earn and redeem on Emirates flights, upgrades, and partner airlines. The lounge access alone (at AED 200-300 per visit if paid) covers the annual fee after 4-5 visits.

Where it falls short:

  • High minimum income (AED 25,000) excludes many residents
  • Annual fee is steep if you don't fly Emirates regularly
  • Miles devalue when redeemed for economy tickets (best value in business/first)

Best for: Frequent Emirates flyers, business travelers, anyone earning AED 25,000+ who wants to upgrade to business class with miles.

Skip if: You fly budget airlines (flydubai, Air Arabia, Wizz Air) or earn under AED 25,000.

2. ADCB Lulu Mastercard — Best for Groceries & Lulu Shopping

Annual fee: AED 315 (first year free) | Min income: AED 10,000/month | Network: Mastercard | Islamic option: Yes

If you do your weekly grocery run at Lulu Hypermarket (and most UAE families do), this card is a no-brainer. You earn 5% cashback on Lulu spends, 2% on groceries elsewhere, and 1% on everything else.

Reward breakdown:

  • Lulu Hypermarket: 5% cashback
  • Other grocery stores (Carrefour, Spinneys, Union Coop): 2% cashback
  • All other spend: 1% cashback
  • Monthly cashback cap: AED 500

Real-world value: A family spending AED 3,000/month at Lulu earns AED 150/month cashback = AED 1,800/year. The annual fee (AED 315 from year 2) is easily covered. First year is free, so you're net positive immediately.

Bonus: 10% off at Lulu Hypermarket on select promotional days, plus financing options on electronics and furniture purchased at Lulu.

Where it falls short:

  • Cashback capped at AED 500/month (high spenders hit the ceiling)
  • 5% rate only applies at Lulu — not useful if you shop elsewhere
  • No travel benefits (no lounge access, no miles)

Best for: Families who shop at Lulu regularly, residents with AED 10,000+ income who want simple, high grocery cashback.

Skip if: You don't shop at Lulu, or you already have a cashback card you're happy with.

3. Mashreq Cashback Platinum — Best Flat-Rate Cashback

Annual fee: AED 525 (often waived first year) | Min income: AED 8,000/month | Network: Visa / Mastercard | Islamic option: Yes

Mashreq's Cashback Platinum is the "one card for everything" pick. Instead of tiered rates that reward specific categories, you earn a flat 1.5% cashback on all spend with no category restrictions, no caps, and no rotating bonus categories to track.

Reward breakdown:

  • All spend: 1.5% flat cashback
  • No monthly cap
  • Cashback credited monthly to card account

Real-world value: Spending AED 10,000/month across all categories = AED 150/month = AED 1,800/year. After the AED 525 annual fee, you net AED 1,275/year. Not the highest ceiling, but the most predictable.

Where Mashreq wins:

  • Lowest income requirement among mid-tier cashback cards (AED 8,000)
  • No category tracking — just swipe and earn
  • Solid mobile app (Mashreq Neo) with real-time cashback tracking
  • No foreign transaction fee on the first AED 5,000/month of international spend

Where it falls short:

  • 1.5% is lower than category-specific cards (ADCB Lulu gives 5% on groceries)
  • Annual fee isn't waived after year 1 (unless you spend enough to negotiate)
  • No travel benefits

Best for: People who want one card for everything, light-to-moderate spenders, anyone who doesn't want to juggle multiple cards for different categories.

Skip if: You spend heavily in one category (groceries, fuel, dining) — a category-specific card earns more.

4. FAB Cashback Visa — Best for Fuel & Utilities

Annual fee: AED 525 (first year free) | Min income: AED 10,000/month | Network: Visa | Islamic option: Yes

First Abu Dhabi Bank (FAB) is the UAE's largest bank, and their Cashback Visa is the best card for drivers and bill-payers. You earn 5% cashback on fuel and 5% on utility bills (DEWA, FEWA, Etisalat, du, and other telecom/utility providers), plus 1% on everything else.

Reward breakdown:

  • Fuel (all petrol stations in UAE): 5% cashback
  • Utility & telecom bills: 5% cashback
  • Groceries: 1% cashback
  • All other spend: 1% cashback
  • Monthly cashback cap: AED 300 on fuel, AED 200 on utilities

Real-world value: Monthly fuel spend of AED 1,500 + utilities of AED 800 = 5% on AED 2,300 = AED 115/month from fuel+utilities alone, plus 1% on other spend. Total annual cashback: AED 1,600-2,000. First year free means net positive from day one.

Where FAB wins:

  • 5% on fuel is the highest in the UAE market (most cards cap at 2-3%)
  • Utility bill cashback is unique — no other card offers this
  • FAB's branch network and customer service are among the best in UAE
  • Supplementary cards free for family members

Where it falls short:

  • Cashback caps are relatively low (AED 300/month on fuel)
  • 1% on non-fuel/utility categories is below average
  • FAB's app is decent but not as polished as Mashreq Neo or Liv

Best for: Car owners, families with high utility bills, anyone who commutes daily in the UAE.

Skip if: You don't drive or your fuel spend is minimal. ADCB Lulu or Mashreq Cashback would be better.

5. HSBC Premier Mastercard — Best Premium Travel Card

Annual fee: AED 1,250 | Min income: AED 40,000/month | Network: Mastercard | Islamic option: No

The HSBC Premier card is for high earners who want a premium travel experience without managing multiple cards. It bundles lounge access, travel insurance, concierge service, and a competitive rewards program into one card — but the income requirement is the highest on this list.

Reward breakdown:

  • All spend: 1 HSBC Reward point per AED 1 (1 point = AED 0.005-0.01 depending on redemption)
  • Travel bookings via HSBC: 3x points
  • Points redeemable for flights, hotels, cashback, or statement credit

Premium benefits:

  • Unlimited Priority Pass lounge access (for cardholder + 1 guest)
  • Comprehensive travel insurance (medical, trip cancellation, lost baggage)
  • Concierge service 24/7
  • Free supplementary cards
  • Global account access (HSBC accounts in 30+ countries)

Real-world value: The lounge access alone (if you travel 10+ times/year) is worth AED 2,000-3,000. The travel insurance (if you'd otherwise buy it separately) saves AED 500-1,000/year. Rewards on AED 20,000/month spend = ~AED 1,200-2,400/year depending on redemption. Total value: AED 4,000-6,000/year, well above the AED 1,250 fee.

Where it falls short:

  • AED 40,000 minimum income excludes most residents
  • Rewards rate (0.5-1%) is lower than dedicated cashback cards
  • You need an HSBC Premier banking relationship (AED 350,000 in deposits or a AED 40,000 salary transfer)

Best for: High earners (AED 40,000+), frequent international travelers, HSBC Premier banking customers, people who value concierge and insurance over raw cashback.

Skip if: You earn under AED 40,000 or don't travel internationally 5+ times per year.

6. Liv. Credit Card — Best Digital / No-Annual-Fee

Annual fee: AED 0 (forever, not just first year) | Min income: AED 5,000/month | Network: Visa | Islamic option: Yes

Liv. (by Emirates NBD) is the digital banking app that also offers a credit card with zero annual fee forever. The rewards aren't the highest, but the absence of a fee means you're always net positive. It's the best "first credit card" in the UAE.

Reward breakdown:

  • Dining & entertainment: 2% cashback
  • Online shopping: 1.5% cashback
  • All other spend: 1% cashback
  • Monthly cashback cap: AED 200

Why Liv. works:

  • Lowest income requirement on this list (AED 5,000)
  • No annual fee — ever
  • Fully digital application (15 minutes via the Liv. app)
  • Instant virtual card for online purchases
  • Integrates with the Liv. app (savings, goals, payments all in one place)

Where it falls short:

  • Low cashback cap (AED 200/month) limits value for high spenders
  • No lounge access or travel benefits
  • Cashback rates are modest compared to category-specific cards
  • No supplementary cards

Best for: First-time credit card users, residents earning AED 5,000-10,000, anyone who wants a no-fee backup card, digital-first users.

Skip if: You spend AED 15,000+/month (cashback cap is too low) or want travel benefits.

7. Citi Cash Back Card — Best for Dining & Entertainment

Annual fee: AED 0 first year (AED 525 from year 2) | Min income: AED 12,000/month | Network: Mastercard | Islamic option: No

Citibank's Cash Back card is the best pick for Dubai's dining and entertainment scene. If you eat out frequently, order delivery via Talabat/Deliveroo, or hit the cinemas and theme parks, this card earns more than any other.

Reward breakdown:

  • Dining (restaurants, cafes, food delivery): 5% cashback
  • Entertainment (cinemas, theme parks, concerts): 5% cashback
  • All other spend: 0.5% cashback
  • Monthly cashback cap: AED 500 on dining+entertainment

Real-world value: Monthly dining spend of AED 2,500 + entertainment AED 500 = 5% on AED 3,000 = AED 150/month = AED 1,800/year. First year is free, so pure profit. From year 2, the AED 525 fee is covered by 4 months of dining cashback.

Where Citi wins:

  • 5% on dining is the best in the UAE (no other card matches this)
  • Talabat, Deliveroo, Zomato, and Careem Now all qualify as dining
  • Citi's global network is useful if you travel (global customer service, online banking worldwide)
  • No foreign transaction fee on international spend

Where it falls short:

  • 0.5% on non-dining/entertainment is terrible — don't use this card for groceries or fuel
  • Not Sharia-compliant (no Islamic option)
  • Citi's UAE presence is smaller than ENBD/FAB/ADCB (fewer ATMs, branches)
  • Annual fee from year 2 if you can't get it waived

Best for: Foodies, young professionals who dine out 4+ times per week, Talabat/Deliveroo power users.

Skip if: You cook at home mostly, or you want a single card for all spending (the 0.5% base rate is too low).

8. Standard Chartered Smart Card — Best Entry-Level Card

Annual fee: AED 0 first year (AED 350 from year 2) | Min income: AED 5,000/month | Network: Visa | Islamic option: Yes

Standard Chartered's Smart Card is the simplest entry into UAE credit cards. Low income requirement, low fee, modest but consistent rewards. If you're new to the UAE, new to credit cards, or just want something basic, this is the card.

Reward breakdown:

  • Online spend: 1% cashback
  • All other spend: 0.5% cashback
  • Monthly cashback cap: AED 1,000

Why it's worth considering:

  • One of the lowest income requirements (AED 5,000)
  • Low annual fee (AED 350, often waived with AED 20,000+ annual spend)
  • Standard Chartered's online banking is solid and reliable
  • Good approval rates for new residents and first-time credit card users
  • Instant approval if you have salary transfer with Standard Chartered

Where it falls short:

  • Reward rates are among the lowest on this list
  • No premium benefits (no lounge, no insurance, no concierge)
  • Cashback credited quarterly, not monthly (slower to see rewards)

Best for: New UAE residents, first-time credit card users, anyone earning AED 5,000-8,000 who doesn't qualify for other cards.

Skip if: You earn AED 10,000+ — you qualify for better cards with higher rewards.

Cashback vs Miles: Which Is Better for You?

This is the most common question, and the answer depends on your lifestyle:

Choose Cashback If:

  • You want predictable, guaranteed value
  • You don't fly 3+ times per year
  • You fly budget airlines (flydubai, Air Arabia, Wizz Air)
  • You prefer simplicity over optimization
  • Your monthly spend is under AED 15,000

Choose Miles If:

  • You fly Emirates or Etihad 3+ times per year
  • You want to redeem for business/first class (where miles are worth 3-5x more)
  • You travel internationally for work
  • You earn AED 25,000+ and don't mind paying an annual fee

The math: AED 10,000/month on a 1.5% cashback card = AED 1,800/year cash. The same spend on Emirates NBD Visa Infinite = ~132,000 Skywards miles/year — enough for a one-way business class ticket Dubai-London (retail value ~AED 12,000-15,000). But only if you actually redeem the miles. Unredeemed miles are worth zero.

Understanding UAE Credit Card Fees

UAE credit cards have several fees that aren't always obvious:

Fee Typical Range What It Means
Annual fee AED 0 - AED 2,500 Yearly card fee. Often waived first year. Negotiable after 1 year of heavy use.
Late payment fee AED 230 - AED 350 Charged if you don't pay the minimum by the due date.
Cash advance fee 3% of amount Withdrawing cash from ATM using your credit card. Avoid — interest accrues immediately.
Foreign transaction fee 1.5% - 3% Charged on non-AED transactions. Some cards (Citi, Mashreq partial) waive this.
Over-limit fee AED 200 - AED 300 Charged if you exceed your credit limit.
Balance transfer fee 1% - 3% Transferring debt from another card. Some banks offer 0% for 3-6 months as a promo.
Supplementary card fee AED 0 - AED 300/card Additional cards for family members. Many premium cards waive this.

The fee that catches most people: The foreign transaction fee. If you travel and spend AED 5,000 abroad, a 3% fee = AED 150 — more than a month's cashback on many cards. Check this before traveling.

How to Choose: A Simple Framework

Step 1: What's your monthly income?

  • Under AED 8,000 → Liv. Credit Card or Standard Chartered Smart Card
  • AED 8,000 - AED 12,000 → Mashreq Cashback Platinum or FAB Cashback
  • AED 12,000 - AED 25,000 → ADCB Lulu, Citi Cash Back, or a combination
  • AED 25,000+ → Emirates NBD Visa Infinite, HSBC Premier, or a multi-card strategy

Step 2: Where do you spend the most?

  • Groceries (Lulu) → ADCB Lulu Mastercard
  • Fuel & utilities → FAB Cashback Visa
  • Dining & delivery → Citi Cash Back Card
  • Emirates flights → Emirates NBD Visa Infinite
  • Everything / mixed → Mashreq Cashback Platinum

Step 3: Do you want one card or two? The optimal UAE strategy is often two complementary cards:

  • ADCB Lulu (5% on groceries) + Citi Cash Back (5% on dining) = max rewards on top categories
  • FAB Cashback (5% on fuel/utilities) + Mashreq Cashback (1.5% on everything else) = full coverage
  • Emirates NBD Visa Infinite (miles on travel) + Liv. Credit Card (0 fee backup) = travel + backup

Step 4: Will you use the travel benefits?

  • Yes (fly 3+ times/year, want lounge access) → Pay the annual fee for a travel card
  • No (rarely fly, fly budget) → Stick with no-fee cashback cards

Step 5: Can you offset the annual fee? Calculate your expected annual cashback or miles value. If it's less than 2x the annual fee, the card isn't worth it. Example: AED 525 fee → you should earn at least AED 1,050 in rewards to justify it.

Common Mistakes UAE Credit Card Users Make

1. Choosing a Card for the Sign-Up Bonus

That free iPad or 25,000 bonus miles looks great — until you realize the annual fee is AED 1,250 and your spending doesn't earn enough to cover it. Pick a card for its ongoing rewards, not the welcome gift.

2. Carrying a Balance

UAE credit card interest rates are 2-3% per month (24-36% annually) — among the highest in the world. If you carry a balance, no amount of cashback will offset it. AED 5,000 carried for a year at 3%/month costs AED 1,800 in interest — more than most cards earn in cashback.

Rule: Pay your full balance every month. Set up auto-pay for the full amount, not the minimum.

3. Using the Wrong Card for the Purchase

If you have an ADCB Lulu card (5% at Lulu, 1% elsewhere) and you swipe it at Carrefour, you earn 1% instead of 5%. Know your card's bonus categories and use the right card for each purchase.

4. Forgetting the Annual Fee Renewal

Banks send a renewal notice 30-60 days before charging the annual fee. If you're not using the card enough to justify it, call and ask for a waiver. Most banks will waive or reduce the fee if you've spent AED 30,000+ in the past year. If they won't, cancel the card.

5. Not Redeeming Rewards

An alarming number of UAE residents have thousands of unredeemed Skywards miles or cashback sitting on their cards. Miles can expire (Skywards miles expire after 3 years of inactivity). Cashback on some cards expires if not redeemed within 12 months. Check your rewards balance monthly and redeem.

6. Applying for Too Many Cards at Once

Each credit card application in the UAE is recorded by the Al Etihad Credit Bureau. Multiple applications in a short period lowers your credit score and can lead to rejections. Apply for one card, wait 3-6 months, then apply for the next.

UAE Credit Card Application Checklist

Before applying, have these ready:

  • Emirates ID (original + copy)
  • Passport copy with valid UAE visa page
  • Salary certificate (from employer, stamped)
  • Bank statements (last 3-6 months, showing salary credits)
  • Trade license (if self-employed)
  • Minimum income proof (salary certificate must show the required amount)

Timeline: 5-10 business days from application to card delivery. Digital-first banks (Liv., Wio) can approve in 24 hours if you already have a salary transfer relationship.

Pro tip: Apply directly through the bank's website or app — not through a mall rep. Online applications sometimes have better welcome offers, and you avoid high-pressure sales tactics.

Saudi Arabia Residents: What's Different?

If you're reading this from Saudi Arabia, the credit card landscape is similar but with key differences:

  • Al Rajhi Bank offers the most popular cashback cards (Signatures, Platinum) with competitive rates and full Sharia compliance.
  • SNB (Saudi National Bank) offers travel and cashback cards with good lounge access across GCC airports.
  • Minimum income requirements are generally lower in KSA (SAR 5,000-15,000 vs. AED 8,000-25,000 in UAE).
  • Mada (Saudi's domestic payment network) is used more widely than Visa/Mastercard for everyday purchases.
  • SAMA (Saudi Central Bank) regulates credit card interest rates, which are capped lower than the UAE.

For a detailed comparison of Saudi banking options, see our Islamic Banking vs Conventional Banking guide.

The Bottom Line

If I had to pick one card for most UAE residents:

  • Budget-conscious (AED 5,000-8,000 income) → Liv. Credit Card. No fee, no stress, modest but guaranteed rewards.
  • Everyday spender (AED 8,000-15,000 income) → Mashreq Cashback Platinum. 1.5% on everything, no category tracking, no caps.
  • Family / grocery-focused → ADCB Lulu Mastercard. 5% on Lulu is unbeatable for weekly grocery runs.
  • Driver / utility-heavy → FAB Cashback Visa. 5% on fuel and bills covers the fee fast.
  • Emirates frequent flyer → Emirates NBD Visa Infinite. Miles + lounge access pays for itself if you fly Emirates.
  • Diner / foodie → Citi Cash Back Card. 5% on dining and Talabat is best-in-class.

The best strategy for many residents is two complementary cards: one for your highest spend category (5% rate) and one flat-rate card (1.5%) for everything else. This maximizes rewards without the complexity of managing 4-5 cards.

Whatever you choose, pay your balance in full every month. The 2-3% monthly interest on UAE credit cards will wipe out any cashback or miles you earn faster than you think.
