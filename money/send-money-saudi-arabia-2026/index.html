How to Send Money from Saudi Arabia to India, Philippines, and Pakistan 2026

2026 comparison of the best ways to send money from Saudi Arabia — fees, exchange rates, speed, and coverage for India, Philippines, Pakistan, and Egypt.

S
SilentEcho Editors
· August 31, 2026

Saudi Arabia is one of the world's largest remittance-sending countries. Millions of expats in Riyadh, Jeddah, Dammam, and other cities send money home every month to India, the Philippines, Pakistan, Egypt, Bangladesh, and other countries. The cost difference between a good transfer service and a bad one can easily be SAR 100-300 per month.

After comparing the major money transfer channels from Saudi Arabia to the main receiving corridors, five options stand out. The best choice depends on whether you want the lowest cost, the fastest speed, cash pickup, or the security of a bank.

This guide compares services and explains costs. It is not financial advice. Exchange rates and fees change daily — always compare the exact rate before sending.

How we compared

We evaluated each service on three practical factors:

  • Total cost: the exchange rate margin plus any fixed fee. A service with zero fees and a poor exchange rate is often more expensive than a service with a visible fee and a fair rate.
  • Speed: how quickly the money arrives, and whether the delivery time is guaranteed or estimated.
  • Receiving network: whether the recipient can get the money in a bank account, mobile wallet, or cash pickup, and how many locations are available in the destination country.

All rates were checked against the mid-market rate on the same day. A competitive service usually keeps the total cost under 1.5-2% for common corridors.

The five picks

1. Wise — Best for bank-to-bank transfers

Wise is the best option if you and your recipient both have bank accounts. It uses the real mid-market exchange rate and charges a small, transparent fee.

What stood out

  • Transparent pricing: the fee is shown upfront before you confirm. For a SAR 5,000 transfer to India, the total cost is usually SAR 25-60 depending on the corridor and payment method.
  • Real exchange rate: Wise converts at the mid-market rate, not a marked-up rate. This is the biggest saving for most users.
  • Fast: most transfers to India, the Philippines, and Pakistan arrive within minutes to a few hours. Bank transfers to Egypt can take 1-2 business days.
  • Regulated: Wise is licensed in Saudi Arabia, the UK, US, and many other jurisdictions.
  • Multi-currency account: you can hold SAR, USD, EUR, GBP, INR, PHP, PKR, EGP, and dozens more.

Where it falls short

  • No cash pickup: Wise cannot send to cash pickup locations. Both sender and recipient need bank accounts.
  • Card payments cost more: paying by card adds a percentage fee. Bank transfer from your Saudi account is cheaper.
  • Not always the cheapest for very small amounts: the fixed fee makes tiny transfers relatively expensive.

Best for: residents with a Saudi bank account sending money to a recipient's bank account abroad. Wise is usually the cheapest option for amounts above SAR 500.

2. STC Pay — Best for Saudi residents with STC accounts

STC Pay is Saudi Arabia's most popular digital wallet. If you already have an STC Pay account linked to your STC mobile number or Saudi bank account, it is one of the easiest ways to send money internationally.

What stood out

  • Integrated with STC: if you are an STC customer, the setup is fast. You can send directly from your STC Pay wallet.
  • International transfer network: STC Pay partners with MoneyGram and other networks to deliver cash pickup and bank transfers to many countries.
  • Cash pickup: recipients in India, the Philippines, Pakistan, and Egypt can often collect cash from partner locations.
  • Mobile-first: the entire process is done through the STC Pay app. No branch visits needed.
  • Competitive rates: STC Pay often offers better rates than traditional exchange houses for common corridors.

Where it falls short

  • Not always the cheapest: the exchange rate margin is usually higher than Wise for the same corridor.
  • Limited to STC ecosystem: if you do not have an STC mobile number or Saudi bank account, setup is harder.
  • Transfer limits: STC Pay has daily and monthly limits that may not suit very large transfers.

Best for: STC customers who want a simple, app-based transfer service with cash pickup options.

3. Al Rajhi Bank / SNB — Best for large transfers and security

Sending money directly from your Saudi bank is not the cheapest option, but it is the safest and most straightforward for large or formal transfers.

What stood out

  • High limits: banks can handle large transfers for property purchases, tuition, business payments, or family support far above the limits of fintech apps.
  • Security and traceability: every transfer is documented and regulated. In case of fraud or error, you have a clear dispute process.
  • Direct from your account: no need to pre-fund a third-party app. You send directly from SAR.
  • SWIFT network: bank wires reach almost every country and bank in the world.
  • Relationship pricing: premium banking customers may get better exchange rates or reduced fees.

Where it falls short

  • High fees: bank wire fees can be SAR 50-150 per transfer plus the receiving bank's fee. Exchange rate margins are usually 1-3%.
  • Slow: international bank transfers take 1-5 business days depending on the corridor and intermediary banks.
  • Paperwork: large transfers may require documentation proving the purpose of the transfer.
  • Not competitive for small amounts: sending SAR 1,000 through a bank is usually more expensive than using Wise or STC Pay.

Best for: large transfers, formal payments, or anyone who prioritises security and traceability over cost.

4. Western Union — Best for global reach

Western Union has the widest physical network in the world. If your recipient lives in a remote area with no bank branch or mobile wallet access, Western Union is often the only realistic option.

What stood out

  • Largest agent network: hundreds of thousands of locations worldwide. In rural India, Pakistan, the Philippines, and Egypt, a Western Union agent may be the closest payout point.
  • Multiple sending options: send online, through the app, from a bank account, debit card, or by visiting an agent location.
  • Multiple receiving options: bank account, cash pickup, mobile wallet, and sometimes home delivery.
  • Speed: cash pickup can be available within minutes after sending from a card or agent location.
  • Reliability: the system has been operating for decades and rarely loses transfers.

Where it falls short

  • Cost: Western Union is often more expensive than Wise or STC Pay for the same corridor. The exchange rate margin can be 2-4% or more, especially for smaller amounts.
  • App experience: the app works but is cluttered and less intuitive than modern fintech apps.
  • Not the best for bank-to-bank: if your recipient has a bank account, Wise is usually cheaper.

Best for: recipients in remote areas with limited banking infrastructure, or senders who need the certainty of a physical agent network.

5. Lulu Exchange / Al Fardan Exchange — Best for cash-to-cash within the GCC

Lulu Exchange and Al Fardan Exchange are major exchange networks in Saudi Arabia with hundreds of branches and strong partnerships in India, the Philippines, Pakistan, and Bangladesh.

What stood out

  • Cash pickup network: both have their own payout locations in many receiving countries. Your recipient can collect cash at a Lulu or Al Fardan branch without needing a bank account.
  • GCC presence: strong network across Saudi Arabia, UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, and Oman.
  • Bank transfers: you can send directly to bank accounts in most major corridors.
  • Familiar process: many long-time Saudi residents are comfortable using exchange houses. Branch staff can walk you through the process.
  • Negotiable rates for large amounts: if you send large amounts regularly, some branches will improve the exchange rate margin.

Where it falls short

  • Exchange rate margin: exchange houses usually add a larger margin than Wise or STC Pay. The total cost is often 1-3% above the mid-market rate.
  • Branch queues: busy weekends and salary days can mean long waits.
  • Less transparent: the exchange rate you see at the branch may not be the best available online. You often have to negotiate or compare.

Best for: people who prefer cash pickup, send money in person, or want a strong branch network in Saudi Arabia and receiving countries.

Hidden costs to watch

The biggest mistake in remittances is looking only at the transfer fee. The real cost is usually in the exchange rate margin.

  • Exchange rate margin: if the mid-market rate is 1 SAR = 22.50 INR, but a service gives you 22.20 INR, you are losing 1.3% on every riyal you send. On SAR 10,000, that is SAR 130 lost to the margin alone.
  • Fixed fees: a low or zero fixed fee can be misleading if the exchange rate margin is high.
  • Receiving fees: some banks in the destination country deduct a fee from the received amount. This is common with bank wires.
  • Card payment fees: paying by credit or debit card often adds 1-3% to the cost. Bank transfer from your Saudi account is usually cheapest.
  • Promotional rates: first-transfer bonuses are nice, but compare the regular rate for your second and third transfers.

Best for specific corridors

  • India: Wise is usually cheapest for bank transfers. STC Pay and Lulu Exchange are strong for cash pickup. Western Union is best for remote areas.
  • Philippines: STC Pay and Wise work well for bank transfers. Lulu Exchange and Western Union are strong for cash pickup.
  • Pakistan: Wise and STC Pay offer good bank transfer options. Lulu Exchange and Western Union are best for cash pickup.
  • Egypt: Bank transfers can be slow due to regulations. Western Union and Lulu Exchange are often better for cash pickup.

The bottom line

For most residents sending money from Saudi Arabia to a bank account abroad, Wise is the cheapest and most transparent option. STC Pay is the best app-based choice for Saudi residents with STC accounts. Al Rajhi Bank or SNB are the safest options for large, formal transfers. Western Union is the fallback when the recipient is in a remote area. Lulu Exchange and Al Fardan Exchange are the most practical for people who prefer to send in person or need cash pickup.

Compare the total cost — fee plus exchange rate margin — every time you send. The cheapest option changes by corridor, amount, and day. Spending 30 seconds comparing can save you SAR 100-300 per month.
