Best Money Transfer Services from UAE 2026: Send Money to India, Philippines, Pakistan, and Egypt

2026 comparison of the best ways to send money from the UAE and Saudi Arabia — fees, exchange rates, speed, and coverage for India, Philippines, Pakistan, and Egypt.

S
SilentEcho Editors
· August 7, 2026

The UAE and Saudi Arabia are two of the largest remittance-sending countries in the world. Millions of expats send money home every month to family, savings accounts, and loan repayments. The difference between a good transfer service and a bad one can easily cost AED 50-200 per month in hidden fees and poor exchange rates.

After comparing the major money transfer channels from the UAE and Saudi Arabia to India, the Philippines, Pakistan, and Egypt — the four most common corridors — five services stood out. The best choice depends on whether you want speed, low cost, cash pickup, or the security of a bank.

This guide compares services and explains costs. It is not financial advice. Exchange rates and fees change frequently — compare the exact rate before sending.

How we compared

We looked at three things that matter for every transfer:

  • Total cost: the exchange rate margin plus the fixed transfer fee. A service with zero fees and a bad exchange rate can be more expensive than a service with a visible fee and a real mid-market rate.
  • Speed and reliability: how quickly the money arrives, whether tracking is clear, and what happens if something goes wrong.
  • Receiving network: whether the recipient can receive the money in a bank account, mobile wallet, or as cash, and how many locations are available in the destination country.

All rates were checked against the mid-market rate on the same day. A "good" service typically keeps the total cost under 1-2% of the transfer amount for common corridors.

The five picks

1. Wise — Best for bank-to-bank transfers

Wise is the best option if you and your recipient both have bank accounts. It uses the real mid-market exchange rate and charges a small, transparent fee. There are no hidden margins.

What stood out

  • Transparent pricing: the fee is shown upfront before you confirm. For a AED 1,000 transfer to India, the total cost is usually AED 10-30 depending on corridor and payment method.
  • Real exchange rate: Wise converts at the mid-market rate, not a marked-up tourist rate. This is the biggest saving for most users.
  • Fast transfers: most transfers to India, the Philippines, and Pakistan arrive within minutes to a few hours. Bank transfers to Egypt can take 1-2 business days.
  • Regulated: Wise is licensed in the UAE, UK, US, and many other jurisdictions. Your money is held in segregated accounts.
  • Multi-currency account: you can hold AED, USD, EUR, GBP, INR, PHP, PKR, EGP, and dozens more. Useful if you want to send later when the rate is better.

Where it falls short

  • Requires a bank account on both sides: Wise cannot send to cash pickup locations. If your recipient needs cash, you need a different service.
  • Not always the cheapest for very small amounts: the fixed fee makes tiny transfers relatively expensive. For amounts under AED 200, other options can be better.
  • Card payments cost more: paying by debit or credit card adds a percentage fee. Bank transfer from your UAE account is the cheapest option.

Best for: residents with a UAE bank account sending money to a recipient's bank account abroad. Wise is usually the cheapest and most transparent option for amounts above AED 300.

2. Remitly — Best for speed and cash pickup

Remitly sits between Wise and traditional cash transfer services. It offers both bank-to-bank transfers and cash pickup, and it is usually faster than traditional exchange houses for home delivery.

What stood out

  • Express and economy options: Express uses a card or faster payment and costs slightly more. Economy uses a bank transfer and is cheaper but takes 3-5 business days.
  • Cash pickup network: thousands of pickup locations in India, the Philippines, Pakistan, and Egypt. Useful if your recipient does not have a bank account or needs cash quickly.
  • Mobile wallet support: in the Philippines, Remitly can send directly to GCash, Maya, and other wallets. This is faster than cash pickup for many recipients.
  • First-time promotions: Remitly often offers a better exchange rate or reduced fees on the first transfer, which can make the first send very cheap.
  • Customer service: 24/7 phone and chat support with agents who understand the main corridors.

Where it falls short

  • Exchange rate margin: Remitly does not always use the mid-market rate. The margin is usually small but can be 0.5-1.5% depending on the corridor and time of day.
  • Express fees add up: if you use Express regularly, the fees can exceed Wise over time.
  • Limited usefulness for very large transfers: Remitly has daily and monthly sending limits that may not suit someone sending large amounts for a property purchase or tuition.

Best for: people who need cash pickup or mobile wallet delivery, and for first-time users who can take advantage of promotional rates.

3. Lulu Exchange / UAE Exchange — Best for cash-to-cash within the GCC

UAE Exchange rebranded as Lulu Exchange after ownership changes. It remains one of the largest exchange networks in the UAE and Saudi Arabia, with hundreds of branches and a strong presence in India, the Philippines, Pakistan, and Bangladesh.

What stood out

  • Cash pickup network: Lulu Exchange has its own payout locations in many receiving countries. Your recipient can collect cash at a Lulu branch or partner location without needing a bank account.
  • GCC presence: strong network across the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, and Oman. Useful if you move between Gulf countries.
  • Bank transfers: you can send directly to bank accounts in most major corridors. Transfers to India and the Philippines are usually same-day or next-day.
  • Familiar process: many long-time UAE residents are comfortable using exchange houses. The branch staff can walk you through the process.
  • Negotiable rates for large amounts: if you send large amounts regularly, some branches will improve the exchange rate margin.

Where it falls short

  • Exchange rate margin: exchange houses usually add a larger margin than Wise or Remitly. The total cost is often 1-3% above the mid-market rate.
  • Branch queues: busy weekends and salary days can mean long waits. The app exists but is not as polished as Wise or Remitly.
  • Less transparent: the exchange rate you see at the branch may not be the best available rate online. You often have to negotiate or compare.

Best for: people who prefer cash pickup, send money in person, or want a strong branch network in the GCC and receiving countries.

4. Western Union — Best for global reach

Western Union is the oldest name in the business and still has the widest physical network. If your recipient lives in a remote area with no bank branch, no mobile wallet, and no app access, Western Union is often the only realistic option.

What stood out

  • Largest agent network: hundreds of thousands of locations worldwide. In rural India, Pakistan, the Philippines, and Egypt, a Western Union agent may be the closest payout point.
  • Multiple sending options: send online, through the app, from a bank account, debit card, or by visiting an agent location.
  • Multiple receiving options: bank account, cash pickup, mobile wallet, and sometimes home delivery.
  • Speed: cash pickup can be available within minutes after sending from a card or agent location.
  • Reliability: the system has been operating for decades and rarely loses transfers.

Where it falls short

  • Cost: Western Union is often more expensive than Wise, Remitly, or Lulu Exchange for the same corridor. The exchange rate margin can be 2-4% or more, especially for smaller amounts.
  • App experience: the app works but is cluttered and less intuitive than modern fintech apps.
  • Not the best for bank-to-bank: if your recipient has a bank account, Wise or Remitly is usually cheaper.

Best for: recipients in remote areas with limited banking infrastructure, or senders who need the certainty of a physical agent network.

5. Bank wire from Emirates NBD, FAB, or Al Rajhi — Best for large transfers and security

Sending money directly from your UAE or Saudi bank is not the cheapest option, but it is the safest and most straightforward for large or formal transfers.

What stood out

  • High limits: banks can handle large transfers for property purchases, tuition, business payments, or family support far above the limits of fintech apps.
  • Security and traceability: every transfer is documented, regulated, and reversible in case of fraud or error.
  • Direct from your account: no need to pre-fund a third-party app. You send directly from AED or SAR.
  • SWIFT network: bank wires reach almost every country and bank in the world.
  • Relationship pricing: if you are a premium banking customer, you may get better exchange rates or reduced fees.

Where it falls short

  • High fees: bank wire fees can be AED 50-100 per transfer plus the receiving bank's fee. Exchange rate margins are usually 1-3%.
  • Slow: international bank transfers take 1-5 business days depending on the corridor and intermediary banks.
  • Paperwork: large transfers may require documentation proving the purpose of the transfer, especially for regulated corridors.
  • Not competitive for small amounts: sending AED 500 through a bank is usually more expensive than using Wise or Remitly.

Best for: large transfers, formal payments, or anyone who prioritises security and traceability over saving a few dirhams.

Hidden costs to watch

The biggest mistake in remittances is looking only at the transfer fee. The real cost is usually in the exchange rate margin.

  • Exchange rate margin: if the mid-market rate is 1 USD = 22.50 INR, but a service gives you 22.25 INR, you are losing 1.1% on every dirham or dollar you send. On AED 5,000, that is AED 55 lost to the margin alone.
  • Fixed fees: a low or zero fixed fee can be misleading if the exchange rate margin is high.
  • Receiving fees: some banks in the destination country deduct a fee from the received amount. This is common with bank wires.
  • Card payment fees: paying by credit or debit card often adds 1-3% to the cost. Bank transfer from your UAE account is usually cheapest.
  • Promotional rates: first-transfer bonuses are nice, but compare the regular rate for your second and third transfers.

Best for specific corridors

  • India: Wise is usually cheapest for bank transfers. Remitly is strong for cash pickup and mobile wallets. Lulu Exchange has a large branch network.
  • Philippines: Remitly is excellent for GCash and Maya mobile wallets. Wise works well for bank transfers. Western Union is best for remote cash pickup.
  • Pakistan: Wise and Remitly both offer good bank transfer options. Lulu Exchange and Western Union are strong for cash pickup.
  • Egypt: Bank transfers can be slow due to banking regulations. Western Union and Lulu Exchange are often better for cash pickup.

The bottom line

For most residents sending money from the UAE or Saudi Arabia to a bank account abroad, Wise is the cheapest and most transparent option. Remitly is the best choice if you need cash pickup or mobile wallet delivery. Lulu Exchange is the most practical for people who prefer to send in person or need a strong GCC payout network. Western Union is the fallback when the recipient is in a remote area with no banking access. Bank wires are the safest option for large, formal transfers.

Compare the total cost — fee plus exchange rate margin — every time you send. The cheapest option changes by corridor, amount, and day. Spending 30 seconds comparing can save you AED 50-200 per month.
