Best Mobile Plans UAE 2026: du vs Etisalat Compared

A practical 2026 comparison of UAE mobile plans — du vs Etisalat postpaid, prepaid, data-heavy, tourist, and budget options.

S
SilentEcho Editors
· August 7, 2026

Getting a mobile plan in the UAE is one of the first things every tourist, resident, and new arrival has to do. You need a local number for everything from food delivery to bank verification to government app notifications. The market is a duopoly: du and Etisalat own essentially the entire consumer mobile market. Virgin Mobile UAE runs as a du brand, and most smaller operators are virtual networks running on one of the two big networks.

After comparing every major plan from both providers in 2026, the choice is not always about which network is better. It is about which plan matches your actual usage. du and Etisalat have different strengths, and the best plan for a tourist is not the best plan for a data-heavy resident.

The two networks: what actually differs

Etisalat (e&)

Etisalat, now branded as e& for its parent company, has the largest physical network in the UAE. It is generally stronger in rural areas, smaller emirates, and buildings with thick concrete walls. If you drive frequently between Dubai and Abu Dhabi, or live in a villa on the edge of the city, Etisalat usually has better coverage.

du

du has a slightly smaller network on paper but performs very well in Dubai, Abu Dhabi, and Sharjah. In many urban areas, du's 5G speeds are faster than Etisalat's. du is also often cheaper for equivalent data allowances and has a more modern app and customer service experience.

The practical difference

For 90% of residents living in Dubai, Abu Dhabi, or Sharjah, the difference is small. Both networks offer fast 5G in most urban areas. Choose du if you want lower cost and better app experience. Choose Etisalat if you travel outside cities or live in an area where du has weak signal.

Best plans for each use case

1. Best overall postpaid plan: du Postpaid Plan 250

The du Postpaid Plan 250 is the best balance of data, allowance, and cost for most residents. It includes:

  • 30 GB local data per month
  • Unlimited local calls within the UAE
  • Flexible contract options: 12-month or 24-month commitment
  • 5G access included
  • Roaming data bundles available as add-ons

At around AED 250/month, it is not the cheapest plan, but it covers most residents without overage anxiety. The 30 GB allowance is enough for navigation, music streaming, messaging, and moderate video use on mobile. Heavy video streamers should look at the higher-tier plan.

The main advantage is du's app, which is cleaner than Etisalat's and makes it easy to track usage, buy add-ons, and manage roaming. The main downside is that du's network can be weaker in rural areas and some older buildings.

Best for: average residents in Dubai or Abu Dhabi who want a reliable all-rounder with a modern app.

2. Best data-heavy plan: Etisalat eLife 100GB or Unlimited

Etisalat's premium postpaid plans are the right choice if you use a lot of mobile data. The 100 GB plan and unlimited data plans are aimed at heavy users who stream video, work on mobile hotspots, or use their phone as a primary internet device.

  • 100 GB to unlimited data depending on the plan tier
  • Unlimited local calls
  • 5G access
  • Better rural and inter-emirate coverage
  • Etisalat Smiles rewards: points that can be redeemed for discounts and vouchers

The unlimited plan is technically unlimited but may have a fair-use policy after a very high threshold. For almost all users, the 100 GB plan is effectively unlimited.

Best for: heavy data users, people who travel frequently between emirates, and those who live in areas with weaker du signal.

3. Best budget plan: du Prepaid Flexi or Virgin Mobile UAE

If you want to spend the minimum, du Prepaid Flexi plans and Virgin Mobile UAE are the best options. Virgin Mobile runs on the du network and offers app-based plan management with no physical store visit required.

A typical budget setup:

  • du Prepaid Flexi: AED 25-50 per month for a smaller data allowance and pay-as-you-go calls.
  • Virgin Mobile UAE: AED 79-129 per month for prepaid bundles with more data and a fully digital experience.

Virgin is particularly useful for new arrivals because the entire signup process is done through the app. You can order a SIM online and activate it with your passport. However, for some government services and account verifications, an Etisalat or du number is sometimes preferred.

Best for: tourists, students, short-term residents, and anyone who wants to keep monthly costs low.

4. Best tourist plan: Etisalat Visitor Line or du Tourist SIM

Tourists can get a SIM at Dubai Airport or most malls. Both operators offer tourist packages with data and local/international calls.

  • Etisalat Visitor Line: usually includes a generous data allowance and international minutes. Good for tourists who need to call home frequently.
  • du Tourist SIM: often cheaper and includes enough data for navigation, messaging, and social media.

Both require a passport for registration. The SIM is valid for the duration of your visa and can be converted to a resident plan if you stay longer.

Best for: tourists staying 1-4 weeks who need a simple, ready-to-use plan.

5. Best family or multi-line plan: Etisalat Family Plans

Etisalat offers family plans that allow multiple SIMs to share a data pool and receive discounts on each line. For a household with two or more active users, this can be cheaper than individual plans.

  • Shared data pool: one large allowance split across multiple numbers
  • Discounted additional lines: usually 20-30% off the second and third lines
  • Unified billing: easier to manage than multiple separate accounts

du also offers multi-line discounts, but Etisalat's family plan structure is generally more flexible for larger households.

Best for: families with 2-5 mobile users who want to share a large data allowance.

Important details to check before signing up

Contract length

Postpaid plans usually require a 12-month or 24-month contract. Early termination incurs a penalty that can be several months of fees. If you are unsure how long you will stay, choose prepaid or a shorter contract.

Roaming

Both du and Etisalat offer daily roaming passes for GCC countries, Europe, and other popular destinations. These are usually cheaper than paying per MB abroad. If you travel frequently, confirm that the roaming pass covers the countries you visit most.

International calls

Most plans include unlimited local calls but charge extra for international calls. WhatsApp voice and video calls are technically restricted in the UAE without a VPN, though many residents use them anyway. For legal calling, both providers offer international minute add-ons. See our guide on VPN legality in the UAE if you are considering a VPN for communication services.

5G coverage

Both networks claim nationwide 5G, but real-world speeds vary by neighbourhood and building. In high-rise apartments and concrete villas, indoor 5G can drop to 4G or LTE. If you live in a large villa, a mesh Wi-Fi system at home will matter more than mobile 5G speed.

Emirates ID requirement

Residents need an Emirates ID to sign up for a postpaid plan. Tourists can use a passport. If you just arrived, you may be on a prepaid plan until your Emirates ID is issued. This is covered in our moving to Dubai checklist.

The bottom line

For most residents in Dubai or Abu Dhabi, du's Postpaid Plan 250 is the best all-rounder. It has enough data, unlimited calls, a modern app, and a reasonable price. If you are a heavy data user or live outside the main cities, Etisalat's premium postpaid plans are worth the extra cost for the better coverage and larger data allowances.

Tourists should grab the cheapest tourist SIM at the airport and convert it to a resident plan later. Budget users and short-term residents should look at du Prepaid Flexi or Virgin Mobile UAE. Families should compare Etisalat's multi-line plans against du's equivalent.

The duopoly means you will not find dramatically cheaper options, but you can avoid paying for data you do not use. Start with a mid-tier plan, track your usage for two months, and then upgrade or downgrade.
