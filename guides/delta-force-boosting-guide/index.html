How to Become a Delta Force (三角洲行动) Boosting and Coaching Partner: Skills, Tools, and How to Find Studios

Delta Force (三角洲行动) is one of the most popular tactical shooters in China and Southeast Asia, with a daily active user base in the tens of millions across PC and mobile. Like other competitive shooters before it (Valorant, CS2, Apex), it has spawned a sizeable boosting / carrying / coaching (护航陪玩) industry — where skilled players are paid to play alongside (or instead of) paying customers.

If you're a strong player considering this as a side income, this guide covers what the work actually looks like, what you need to bring to the table, how to find legitimate studios and clubs, and the risks nobody talks about.

Quick note on terminology: "护航" (hù háng) literally means "escort / protect," and refers to higher-skilled players protecting or carrying a paying customer through a match. "陪玩" (péi wán) means "play alongside" and covers everything from casual duo-queueing to dedicated coaching. The two overlap a lot in practice.

What the Work Actually Looks Like

There are roughly three tiers of work available:

Tier 1: Casual Coaching / Duo-Queue Partner

The most legitimate and lowest-risk option. You play alongside a customer in their ranked games, give callouts, share positioning, and answer questions. You're a friend who happens to be very good at the game.

  • Typical pay: ¥30-80 / hour (~$4-11 USD)
  • Time commitment: Flexible, by the hour
  • Skill required: Decent (high gold or platinum rank in Delta Force's ranked mode)
  • Tools needed: A good mic, Discord or in-game voice, fast reflexes

Tier 2: Rank Boosting / Carrying

You log into the customer's account (or play on their account) and grind ranked matches to a specific target rank — for example, from Gold to Diamond. The customer pays a fixed fee per tier.

  • Typical pay: ¥30-200 / star / rank tier (varies wildly — Diamond is more expensive than Silver)
  • Time commitment: 1-4 hours per order
  • Skill required: Very high (Master+ rank)
  • Tools needed: Fast stable internet, secondary device or emulator setup for the customer's account, often a VPN if playing cross-region

Tier 3: Tournament / Clan / Sponsored Play

You join a competitive clan, grind for tournament prize money, or get sponsored by a studio. The most skilled and visible players fall here.

  • Typical pay: ¥3,000-30,000 / month in stable income, plus prize pools
  • Time commitment: Full-time (often 8-12 hours / day)
  • Skill required: Top 0.1% server rank, often with streaming/visibility requirements
  • Tools needed: Top-tier PC, multi-monitor, capture card, professional mic/camera, often a dedicated studio space

Most people start in Tier 1 and move up as they build a reputation and a client base.

The Skills You Actually Need

Being "good at the game" is the floor, not the ceiling. Here's what separates the people who can do this professionally from those who struggle:

1. Mechanical Skill (non-negotiable for Tier 2+)

  • Aim consistency: you should be able to land shots reliably in a variety of scenarios (close range, long range, while moving)
  • Game sense: knowing where enemies are likely to be, predicting rotations, controlling high-traffic map areas
  • Movement: slide-cancelling, peek-and-flick mechanics, recoil control on every weapon class
  • Operator knowledge: every operator's abilities, cooldowns, and counter-play

The bar is roughly: top 5% of ranked players in your server to start. You can audit yourself by looking at your last 50 ranked games — if your win rate is below 65% at your current rank, you're not ready yet.

2. Communication (critical for all tiers)

  • Clear, concise callouts: "Two pushing B, one with a sniper on the back line"
  • Calm under pressure: customers get tilted, your job is to keep them calm and playing well
  • Bilingual (for some studios): many Chinese studios take both domestic and SEA customers, so basic English callouts are valued

3. Reliability and Professionalism

This is the part most new players underestimate. Studios and direct clients care far more about reliability than raw skill:

  • Show up on time for scheduled sessions
  • Stay the full duration even if the match is going badly
  • Be honest about your skill level — don't promise Diamond if you're only Platinum
  • Have a stable internet connection (dropouts lose you repeat business)

4. Mental Stamina

Carrying weaker players is mentally exhausting in a way that playing at your own level is not. You're making 5x the decisions per second, tracking your own positioning and theirs, and the matches are far less predictable. Burnout is real — most people can sustain 4-6 hours a day, few can do 10+.

Hardware and Software Setup

The Basics (under ¥10,000 / $1,400)

  • A PC that can run Delta Force at 144+ FPS on competitive settings: typically an RTX 3060 or above, 16GB RAM, modern Intel/AMD CPU
  • A 144Hz+ monitor with low response time (1ms is ideal)
  • A quality headset with a clear mic (HyperX, Beyerdynamic, Sennheiser all work)
  • A second device (cheap laptop or tablet) for the customer's account in Tier 2 work

The Pro Setup (¥20,000-50,000 / $2,800-7,000)

  • 240Hz+ monitor (Zowie, ASUS ROG, Alienware)
  • Mechanical keyboard with optical switches (low latency)
  • Lightweight mouse (under 70g) with optical switches
  • Capture card if streaming
  • Webcam and ring light if doing video coaching
  • A second PC (often called a "slave") for managing multiple accounts simultaneously

Software

  • Discord — universal for communication
  • OBS Studio — for streaming or recording
  • Delta Force's official tools — built-in replay system is good for reviewing your own gameplay
  • A macro/policy tool (controversial — see risks section below)
  • Voice changer (if you want privacy or anonymity)

How to Find Legitimate Studios and Clubs

This is where most beginners lose time or get scammed. Here's a tiered approach:

Level 1: Verified Platforms

Several platforms specifically match boosting customers with coaches in China. They handle payment, dispute resolution, and have rating systems:

  • 比心 (BiXin) — one of the largest 陪玩 platforms, with explicit Delta Force support
  • 捞月狗 (LaoYueGou) — similar model, also covers multiple games
  • TT 语音 (TT Voice) — originally a social voice app, now has a substantial 陪玩 marketplace

On these platforms, you sign up as a "陪玩" (companion player), pass a skill verification (usually requires uploading screenshots of your rank and recent match history), and then list yourself for customers to find. The platform takes a 10-20% cut of your earnings.

Pros: Safer than going direct, disputes are handled for you, ratings help you build reputation Cons: Lower per-hour rates (because of the cut), and platform rules can be strict

Level 2: Studio Direct

Studios (工作室) hire boosting partners more directly. They typically have:

  • A manager who assigns orders
  • A WeChat or QQ group for coordination
  • Set schedules (often 4-hour blocks)
  • Higher per-hour rates (¥80-200 / hour) but stricter requirements

To find these:

  • WeChat groups: Search "三角洲陪玩" or "三角洲工作室" — many studios recruit via WeChat
  • QQ groups: Similar search terms on QQ
  • Bilibili / Douyin (TikTok China): Some studios actively recruit through short videos
  • Discord servers: International (mostly SEA-based) studios recruit on Discord

Be cautious: legitimate studios never charge an upfront fee to join. If someone asks for a "deposit" or "training fee," it's almost certainly a scam.

Level 3: Direct Clients

Once you have a reputation, customers will find you directly. Many Tier 1 / Tier 2 coaches report that 70%+ of their long-term income comes from repeat direct clients who pay slightly above market rate for someone they trust.

Building this takes time — usually 3-6 months of consistent Tier 1 work on a platform to accumulate ratings and reviews.

How Much Can You Realistically Make?

Honest ranges, based on 2025-2026 Chinese market data:

Tier Hours/week Monthly income (CNY) Monthly income (USD)
Tier 1: Casual 5-10 ¥600-3,200 $80-450
Tier 2: Rank carry 10-20 ¥3,000-12,000 $420-1,700
Tier 3: Sponsored 30-40 ¥10,000-30,000+ $1,400-4,200+

For comparison, full-time minimum wage in tier-1 Chinese cities is around ¥3,000-4,000/month. Tier 2 boosting at 10-15 hours/week is comparable to a part-time job in earnings, with the trade-off of irregular hours and no benefits.

The Risks Nobody Talks About

1. Account Ban Risk

This is the biggest risk. Game publishers (including Tencent for Delta Force) explicitly prohibit:

  • Playing on someone else's account
  • Selling boosting services
  • "Account sharing" in any form

If caught, the customer's account, your account, and potentially your hardware ID can all be banned. The detection methods are getting better every year — behavior analysis, IP tracking, device fingerprinting, and pattern detection.

How to mitigate: Never use the same hardware / IP for your own account and a customer's account on the same game. Use a separate device and connection.

2. Payment Scams

Especially on WeChat / QQ direct deals, scams are common:

  • Customer pays, you complete the order, they reverse the payment (chargeback)
  • Customer pays via fake transfer screenshot
  • Studio withholds payment for "disputes" that never existed
  • Customer blackmails you (e.g. "I'll report you for account theft" unless you refund)

How to mitigate: Always use a platform for first-time orders with new customers. Never accept payment before completing the order. Screenshot everything.

3. Physical and Mental Health

  • Long sessions cause RSI (wrist, shoulder, neck) — invest in ergonomics early
  • Eye strain — get blue-light glasses, take 5-minute breaks per hour
  • Mental burnout — schedule at least 1-2 full days off per week
  • Social isolation — most of your work is solo gaming; make time for in-person relationships

4. Legal Gray Areas

In China, "陪玩" sits in a legal gray area. It's not illegal per se, but the moment it crosses into:

  • Account theft (using stolen accounts)
  • Money laundering (fake transactions)
  • Match fixing (deliberately throwing games for gambling)

...it becomes a criminal matter. Stay away from anything that looks like any of these.

5. Tax and Legal Status

In China, money earned as a 陪玩 is technically self-employment income and should be declared. Most platforms will issue payment records that match your bank account. Direct WeChat deals are harder to track but riskier if you ever have a tax audit.

A Reasonable First-30-Days Plan

If you're starting from zero and want to do this responsibly:

Week 1: Reach Master rank in Delta Force on your main account. Make sure you actually have the skill to back this up — don't lie to yourself.

Week 2: Set up your basic hardware (good mic, dual monitor if possible) and create accounts on 2-3 platforms (比心, 捞月狗, TT 语音).

Week 3: Get verified on the platforms. Pass the skill tests (most require a screenshot of your recent ranked history). Set your hourly rate at the low end of market to build reviews.

Week 4: Accept 5-10 short sessions. After each, ask for a review. Adjust your rate based on demand.

Month 2 onwards: Build reputation, raise rates gradually, develop regular clients. By month 3-6, you should know if this is sustainable for you or not.

The Bottom Line

Delta Force boosting is real work with real pay, but it's also a crowded market with significant risks. The people who succeed long-term treat it professionally: they show up on time, communicate well, and build reputations over years, not weeks.

If you have a Master+ rank, a reliable setup, and a few hours a day to commit, it's a legitimate way to monetize a skill you already have. Just be realistic about the ceiling — most people cap out at the equivalent of part-time work, and the burnout rate is real.

If you want to play games for a living, competitive play (Tier 3) is the better long-term path if you have the skill — prize pools, sponsorships, and streaming income all scale higher than per-hour boosting.
