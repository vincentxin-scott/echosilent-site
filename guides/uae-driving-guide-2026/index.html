How to Get a UAE Driving License in 2026: A Complete Guide for Expats

If you've just moved to the UAE, one of the first practical things on your list is figuring out how to legally drive. Unlike many countries, you can't just rent a car or hand over your home-country license. The UAE has a strict, multi-step licensing process that's administered at the emirate level, and the steps and costs vary slightly between Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Sharjah, and the other five emirates.

This guide walks through the full 2026 process for expats: who is eligible, what documents you need, how to choose a driving school, what each test costs, and how long the whole thing takes. It's based on current rules from the RTA (Dubai) and the Department of Municipalities and Transport (Abu Dhabi), plus what real expats actually experience on the ground.

Geographic note: This guide covers Dubai and Abu Dhabi in detail (where 90% of expats live), with notes for other emirates at the end. Rules in Saudi Arabia are similar but separate (driving licenses issued by the Saudi General Directorate of Traffic; women have been allowed to drive since 2018).

Who Needs a UAE License?

You need a UAE driving license if:

  • You're a UAE resident (with a valid residency visa) and you want to drive legally in the UAE.
  • You're a tourist or short-term visitor — you can drive with a home-country license, an International Driving Permit (IDP), or a GCC license for up to 6 months in most emirates. After that, you need a UAE license.

Important exceptions:

  • GCC citizens (Saudi, Emirati, Kuwaiti, Qatari, Bahraini, Omani) can drive on their home-country license indefinitely — no UAE license required.
  • Citizens of 32+ approved countries (US, UK, EU, Canada, Australia, Japan, Singapore, etc.) can directly convert their home-country license to a UAE license without taking the full test — but only if their home license is current and valid.
  • Everyone else must take the full process: theory test, driving school hours, road test, and (in some cases) a final assessment.

The "approved countries" list changes occasionally, so check the current RTA or DMT website before assuming you qualify for direct conversion.

Step 0: Gather Your Documents

Before you start anything, make sure you have:

  1. UAE residency visa (stamped in your passport). Tourist visas don't qualify.
  2. Emirates ID (the plastic card, not just the application receipt).
  3. Home-country driving license (if you have one — even an expired one can sometimes be useful for skipping the beginners' theory test).
  4. Passport copy + visa page copy.
  5. Photos (usually provided by the driving school at registration).
  6. No-objection letter (NOC) from your sponsor (employer), if your employer is paying for lessons or if you're on a company visa. Not required for all expats.
  7. Eye test (can be done at the driving school or at any RTA/DMT-approved optician — usually 100-150 AED).

If you're converting from an approved country, you'll also need a certificate of authenticity for your home license (a translation + attestation from the UAE embassy in your home country + MOFA in the UAE). This is the most expensive and time-consuming part for direct conversion — budget 2-4 weeks and 800-1500 AED if you need it done professionally.

Step 1: Choose a Driving School

Each emirate has multiple approved driving schools. In Dubai, the main ones are:

  • Belhasa Driving Center — Largest, multiple branches, generally the busiest. Higher throughput but busier classrooms.
  • Galadari Motor Driving Centre (GMDC) — Premium option, smaller classes, more flexible scheduling.
  • Dubai Driving Centre (DDC) — Mid-tier, good reputation with Asian expats especially.
  • Al Ahli Driving Center — Budget-friendly, slower pace.

In Abu Dhabi:

  • Abu Dhabi Driving School (ADDS) — Main option.
  • Al-Ain Driving Training Center — For Al Ain residents.

Choose based on:

  • Schedule flexibility: GMDC and ADDS typically offer more evening and weekend slots.
  • Cost per hour: ranges from 80 AED to 250 AED depending on school and transmission type (automatic vs manual). Automatic is usually 20-30% cheaper.
  • Location: Most branches are in the main city; factor in commute time.
  • Reviews: Google Reviews are more reliable than the school's own marketing. Look for "passed first time" rates if you can find them.

Step 2: Open a Training File

Once you've picked a school, you'll:

  1. Submit your documents (the school scans and uploads them to the licensing authority's system).
  2. Pay the registration fee (typically 200-450 AED).
  3. Get a "training file number" — this is what the licensing authority tracks you under.
  4. Schedule your first theory class.

In Dubai, the licensing authority is the RTA (Roads and Transport Authority) and the file is opened at one of their customer service centers or via the school.

Step 3: Pass the Theory Test

Before you can drive on real roads, you need to pass a theory test. It covers:

  • Traffic signs and signals (the biggest section — 60+ signs to memorize).
  • Right-of-way rules (UAE-specific: roundabouts, sand on roads, camels, etc.).
  • Speed limits (varies by road type — 60 km/h urban, 80-100 km/h on most highways, 120 km/h on Sheikh Zayed Road).
  • Defensive driving basics.
  • Emergency procedures (breakdowns, accidents, sandstorms, fog).
  • First aid basics.

Format: 35-40 multiple-choice questions, computer-based. Need ~70% to pass. Available in English, Arabic, Hindi, Urdu, and several other languages.

Study resources:

  • The official RTA theory handbook (free PDF download from the RTA website, or available at the school).
  • The RTA Smart App has practice tests (free).
  • YouTube videos for visual learners — search "UAE RTA theory test" for 2024-2026 walkthroughs.
  • The school's theory classes (usually 8-16 hours total, can be done in person or online).

Cost: 200-300 AED for the test booking.

Tip: Don't underestimate the theory test. First-time fail rates are around 25-30%, mostly because people skip the studying. Spend 2-3 evenings with the handbook before scheduling.

Step 4: Complete the Practical Training Hours

Once you pass theory, you start on-road training. The required hours depend on your license history:

Background Required hours
Never held any license 40 hours (some schools offer 30-hour fast track)
Home license from non-approved country (expired 5+ years) 20-40 hours
Home license from non-approved country (recent) 15-20 hours
Direct conversion from approved country 0 hours (skip training, take final assessment only)

What you'll learn:

  • Vehicle controls (in a training car with dual brakes)
  • Basic maneuvers (parking, three-point turn, hill start)
  • City driving (Dubai/Abu Dhabi traffic is famously aggressive — your instructor will prepare you)
  • Highway driving (lane discipline at 120 km/h, dealing with tailgaters)
  • Night driving (a few sessions)
  • Defensive driving (the UAE has a high accident rate, instructors emphasize this)

Cost: 80-250 AED per hour depending on school, transmission (auto/manual), and whether it's a 1-on-1 or small-group session.

Time commitment: Most expats complete hours in 2-4 months with 2-3 sessions per week.

Step 5: Pass the Road Test (Final Assessment)

Once your instructor signs you off as ready, you book the road test. This is the moment of truth.

Format:

  • ~20 minutes of driving with an RTA/DMT examiner.
  • Includes: highway entry/exit, parallel parking, reverse parking, U-turns, traffic light compliance, and lane discipline.
  • The examiner sits in the back seat and grades you on a checklist.
  • Pass rate: around 50-60% on first attempt.

Common failure reasons:

  • Not checking mirrors before lane changes (the #1 reason).
  • Rolling stops at stop signs.
  • Speeding even by 5 km/h.
  • Cutting corners on U-turns.
  • Not using turn signals.
  • Sudden braking.
  • Indecision at roundabouts.

Cost: 300-500 AED for the test booking (separate from training fees).

If you fail: You can rebook after a waiting period (usually 1-2 weeks) and may need extra training hours. The school will usually give you a free 2-hour refresher if you fail.

Step 6: Get Your License

After passing, you:

  1. Pay the license issuance fee (~200 AED for 2-year license, ~400 AED for 10-year).
  2. Submit your photo and signature (done at the licensing authority center).
  3. Wait 1-3 business days for the plastic card to be ready (or pick it up the same day at some centers).

Your new UAE driving license is valid for 2 years (under-21 drivers) or up to 10 years (21+ drivers, renewable).

Other Emirates (Sharjah, Ajman, UAQ, RAK, Fujairah)

The process is similar but:

  • Sharjah: Slightly cheaper (driving schools are ~10-20% less than Dubai). Less traffic, easier road test in many cases.
  • Ajman, UAQ, RAK, Fujairah: Smallest driving schools, less flexibility in scheduling, but lower costs.
  • Cross-emirate license: A Dubai license is valid across all UAE emirates; same for Abu Dhabi. You don't need a separate license for each.

If you live in Sharjah but work in Dubai, you can get a Sharjah license — it's valid everywhere in the UAE.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  1. Skipping the theory study. The handbook is 80+ pages but the test pulls from every chapter. People who fail twice usually didn't study the first time.
  2. Booking the road test too early. Your instructor will tell you when you're ready — listen to them. Re-tests cost money and time.
  3. Choosing manual transmission just to "save money". Automatic is now the standard in the UAE (95%+ of rental cars and most personal cars are automatic). Save yourself the hassle unless you specifically need manual for a job.
  4. Not practicing enough outside school hours. You can rent a car (with a temporary permit) to practice with a friend who has a UAE license. The school's hours are rarely enough alone.
  5. Going to the cheapest school without checking reviews. Cheap schools sometimes have older cars, less experienced instructors, and lower pass rates — which means more re-tests and total higher cost.

After You Get Your License

A few things to know:

  • Car insurance is mandatory. Basic third-party starts at ~800 AED/year; comprehensive is 2,500-5,000 AED depending on car value and your driving history.
  • Salik is Dubai's toll system (4 AED per gate). Most cars have a Salik tag now. Don't try to dodge tolls — they add up and the fine is 100 AED per unpaid toll.
  • Parking: paid parking zones are everywhere in Dubai and Abu Dhabi. Use the RTA/DMT apps to pay (mPay, DARB, or the RTA app).
  • Renting a car: requires UAE license + passport + credit card. Minimum age is usually 21 (25 for premium cars).
  • Buying a car: worth it if you drive >15,000 km/year. Otherwise rent or use Careem/Uber (much more common in UAE than ownership for expats).
