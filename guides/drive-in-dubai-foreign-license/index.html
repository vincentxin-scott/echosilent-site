Can I Drive in Dubai with a UK, US, or Indian Licence?

A clear guide to driving in Dubai on a foreign licence, including licence-swap countries, International Driving Permits, and when you must get a UAE licence.

S
SilentEcho Editors
· July 31, 2026

One of the first practical questions new arrivals in Dubai ask is whether they can drive legally on their home country licence. The answer depends on where your licence was issued, whether you are a tourist or resident, and how long you plan to stay.

The short version:

  • Tourists and visit-visa holders can usually drive on a valid foreign licence plus an International Driving Permit (IDP), depending on their nationality.
  • Residents must convert to a UAE driving licence within a set period. Some countries have a swap agreement that makes this instant; others require driving lessons and a road test.

Tourists and visit-visa holders

If you are in Dubai on a tourist visa, you can drive a rental car using:

  • A valid licence from your home country
  • An International Driving Permit (IDP) that matches the licence
  • A passport that matches both documents

The rental company will check these. Most international licences are accepted, but the exact rules depend on the country that issued your licence. Always check with the rental company before booking, especially if your licence is not in English or Arabic.

Do UK, US, and Indian tourists need an IDP?

  • UK licence holders: yes, an IDP is recommended and often required by rental companies. The UK licence alone may not be accepted for car rentals in Dubai.
  • US licence holders: an IDP is recommended, but many rental companies accept a US licence alone. Check with the specific company.
  • Indian licence holders: an IDP is usually required. Some rental companies accept an Indian licence for short-term rentals, but this is not guaranteed.

An IDP is a translation document, not a standalone licence. It must be carried together with your original licence. You must obtain it in your home country before travelling — you cannot get an IDP once you are in Dubai.

Residents: when you must switch to a UAE licence

Once you become a UAE resident, you cannot continue driving on a foreign licence indefinitely. The exact rules vary by emirate, but in Dubai the general rule is:

  • If your home country has a licence-swap agreement with the UAE, you can convert your licence without lessons or a test. You will need your original licence, passport, Emirates ID, residence visa, and a passport photo.
  • If your home country does not have a swap agreement, you must attend driving lessons and pass a theory test, parking test, and road test at an authorised driving school.

The process usually takes a few hours if you can swap, and several weeks if you need lessons and tests.

Countries with UAE licence-swap agreements

The list changes occasionally, but as of 2026 it includes most of Western Europe, North America, Australia, New Zealand, and several East Asian countries. Commonly accepted countries include:

  • United Kingdom
  • United States
  • Canada
  • Australia
  • New Zealand
  • Germany
  • France
  • Italy
  • Spain
  • Netherlands
  • Belgium
  • Switzerland
  • Austria
  • Denmark
  • Sweden
  • Norway
  • Finland
  • Ireland
  • Portugal
  • Greece
  • Japan
  • South Korea
  • Singapore
  • Hong Kong

India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, and most African countries are generally not on the swap list. Drivers from these countries usually need to take the full UAE driving course and tests.

Always confirm the current list with the Dubai Roads and Transport Authority (RTA) before planning, because agreements change.

What if my licence is not on the swap list?

If you hold an Indian, Pakistani, Bangladeshi, or other non-swap licence, you will need to:

  1. Register with an RTA-approved driving school (e.g., Emirates Driving Institute, Galadari, Belhasa).
  2. Complete the required number of classes. This varies by your existing experience and the school's assessment.
  3. Pass the theory test.
  4. Pass the parking test.
  5. Pass the road test.
  6. Pay the licence issuance fee and receive your UAE licence.

The full process typically takes 4-8 weeks and costs AED 4,000-7,000 depending on the school and how many extra classes you need.

Should you buy a car immediately or rent first?

Most expats make the mistake of buying a car in their first month. The better approach is to rent for 2-3 months while you learn the roads, understand your commute, and decide whether you actually need a car.

Renting a car in Dubai costs AED 1,200-2,500 per month for a basic sedan, usually including insurance and maintenance. This is cheaper than the depreciation of a new car in the first year, and it removes the risk of buying the wrong vehicle for your actual needs.

Buying a car makes sense if you plan to stay 2+ years and drive daily. A new Toyota Corolla starts around AED 65,000, but it loses 20-30% of its value in the first year. A second-hand car from a reputable dealer can be better value if you know what to check.

Leasing is a middle ground. Long-term leases run AED 1,500-3,000 per month and include insurance, maintenance, and registration. For many expats staying 2-3 years, leasing beats buying.

Car insurance: what new drivers need to know

Car insurance is mandatory in Dubai. For a new arrival, premiums are higher than for residents with a UAE licence history.

  • Third-party insurance: the legal minimum. Covers damage you cause to others, not your own car. Costs roughly AED 1,000-2,500 per year.
  • Comprehensive insurance: covers your own car too. Costs AED 2,500-5,000+ per year depending on the car value and your driving history.
  • No-claims discount: if you have a clean driving record from your home country, some insurers may offer a discount, but many do not recognise foreign no-claims certificates.

Shop around. Insurance prices vary significantly between providers, and online aggregators in the UAE can save you 20-30% compared to walking into a single insurer's office.

Driving in Dubai: what to expect

Dubai roads are generally excellent — wide motorways, clear signage, and modern traffic systems. However, driving culture is different from the UK, US, or India:

  • Speed limits are higher: 100-120 km/h on many main roads. Speed cameras are everywhere and fines are automatic.
  • Lane discipline is loose: drivers often change lanes without indicating, and undertaking is common.
  • Salik tolls: AED 4 per toll gate on Sheikh Zayed Road and other major routes. The fee is deducted automatically if your car has a Salik tag.
  • Roundabouts: give way to traffic already in the roundabout, not to traffic entering.
  • Phone use: using a phone while driving is illegal and heavily fined.
  • Zero tolerance for drink-driving: the legal blood alcohol limit is effectively zero.

If you are not confident driving in fast, aggressive traffic, take a few lessons with a local instructor even if you can swap your licence. The roads are safe, but the driving style can be intimidating at first.

The bottom line

Tourists can drive on a foreign licence plus an IDP. Residents must convert to a UAE licence. If you are from the UK, US, Canada, Australia, or most of Western Europe, you can usually swap your licence instantly. If you are from India, Pakistan, or most other countries, you will need to take lessons and tests.

Check the RTA website or call an RTA-approved driving school for the most up-to-date list of swap-eligible countries.
