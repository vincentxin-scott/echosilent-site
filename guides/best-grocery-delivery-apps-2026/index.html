Best Grocery Delivery Apps in UAE and Saudi Arabia 2026

2026's best grocery delivery apps in the Gulf — compared on price, speed, product range, and fees for UAE and Saudi Arabia.

S
SilentEcho Editors
· July 28, 2026

Grocery delivery is not a luxury in the Gulf for most of the year. From May to September, walking from a car to a supermarket entrance can feel like stepping into a hair dryer. Add weekend traffic, mall parking, and the fact that many families live in villas 15 minutes from the nearest decent supermarket, and delivery becomes the default.

After ordering from every major grocery app in the UAE and Saudi Arabia over three months — Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Riyadh, and Jeddah — here are the five apps that actually deliver on their promises, plus the two that look cheap but cost more once you read the fine print.

How we compared

We evaluated each app across four things that actually matter:

  • Total basket cost: product price + delivery fee + service fee + tip. Some apps mark up individual items more than others.
  • Delivery speed: scheduled slot vs on-demand, peak-hour delays, and whether the app hits the promised window.
  • Product range: fresh produce quality, international brands, halal options, and substitutions.
  • Reliability: order accuracy, refund speed, and customer service response time.

We placed identical orders where possible — a basket of 15 staple items including milk, eggs, bread, chicken, rice, vegetables, and cleaning products — to compare real prices rather than advertised fees.

The five picks

1. Talabat Mart — Best overall in the UAE

Talabat Mart is the most reliable grocery delivery option in the UAE. It sits inside the Talabat super-app, which most residents already have installed for restaurant delivery. The grocery arm is fast, well-stocked, and surprisingly consistent.

What stood out

  • Speed: on-demand delivery in 30-60 minutes in Dubai, Abu Dhabi, and Sharjah. In our tests, 80% of orders arrived within the promised window.
  • Range: partners with Carrefour, Lulu, and smaller local stores. You can switch between partner stores inside the app, which matters because prices vary.
  • Fees: delivery AED 7-12, service fee ~AED 2-4. No hidden markup on most Carrefour items when ordered through Talabat Mart.
  • Substitutions: the app prompts you to approve replacements before the rider leaves the store. If they don't, refunds are automatic and usually processed within 24 hours.

Where it falls short

  • Not available in Saudi Arabia. Talabat operates restaurants in KSA but Talabat Mart is UAE-only. If you're in Riyadh or Jeddah, skip this one.
  • Peak-hour delays: Friday 5-8 pm can stretch 60-minute slots to 90 minutes.
  • Fresh produce quality depends on partner: ordering from a Carrefour partner store gives better veg than a small convenience partner.

Best for: UAE residents who want one app for both restaurant and grocery delivery. If you already use Talabat for food, adding groceries is frictionless.

2. Noon Daily — Best value in the UAE and Saudi Arabia

Noon Daily is Noon's grocery vertical. It works in both the UAE and Saudi Arabia, and it's consistently the cheapest option for packaged goods, cleaning supplies, and pantry staples when you factor in promotions and Noon Yellow Friday-style sales.

What stood out

  • Price: 5-15% cheaper than Carrefour on average for branded packaged goods. Own-brand "Noon Daily" products are 20-30% cheaper than name brands for basics like rice, oil, and cleaning products.
  • Subscription: Noon One (AED 18/month or SAR 19/month) gives free delivery on Noon Daily, Noon, and Noon Food. If you order twice a month, it pays for itself.
  • Coverage: UAE and Saudi Arabia. Works well in Riyadh, Jeddah, Dammam, Dubai, Abu Dhabi, and Sharjah.
  • Scheduled slots: good for planning. You can book next-day delivery in 2-hour windows, often with free delivery above a minimum spend.

Where it falls short

  • Fresh produce is inconsistent. Noon Daily sources from local partners, and quality varies by city and warehouse. We've received excellent tomatoes in Riyadh and bruised cucumbers in Dubai.
  • Customer service is slow. Refunds and missing-item complaints can take 48-72 hours.
  • The app is cluttered. Noon tries to sell you electronics, fashion, and groceries at once. Grocery search is sometimes buried under promotions.

Best for: families who buy pantry staples in bulk and want the lowest total basket cost. Also the best choice if you live in both countries and want one app to learn once.

3. Kibsons — Best fresh produce in the UAE

Kibsons is a UAE-only grocery delivery service that started as a fresh produce box company. It still owns that identity: the fruit, vegetables, and meat are the best in this list, and the packaging is designed to keep things cold in the back of a delivery van during summer.

What stood out

  • Fresh produce quality: consistently better than Carrefour, Lulu, and Noon Daily. Leafy greens arrived crisp, berries weren't bruised, and meat was properly cold-chained.
  • Organic and specialty: wide range of organic produce, keto products, gluten-free, and imported items that are hard to find elsewhere.
  • Sustainable packaging: less plastic than competitors, and the chilled bags are reusable.
  • Delivery: same-day or next-day slots in Dubai, Abu Dhabi, and Sharjah. Free delivery over AED 100.

Where it falls short

  • Expensive: fresh produce costs 15-30% more than Carrefour. A weekly vegetable shop that costs AED 80 at Carrefour can cost AED 110 at Kibsons.
  • Limited range: it's not a full supermarket. Cleaning products, toiletries, and packaged goods are limited.
  • UAE-only: no Saudi coverage.

Best for: households that prioritise food quality over price, especially if you cook fresh meals daily. Also ideal for families with dietary restrictions or people who want organic produce delivered.

4. Amazon.ae / Amazon.sa — Best for pantry and subscribe-and-save

Amazon's grocery offering in the Gulf is less famous than its US counterpart, but it's the best place to buy non-perishable staples in bulk. Rice, oil, canned goods, detergents, coffee, and pet food are often cheaper here than on any grocery app.

What stood out

  • Subscribe & Save: set up recurring deliveries and save 5-15% on staples. Useful for items you buy every month: coffee, diapers, detergent, oil, rice.
  • Bulk pricing: 5-litre oil, 10-kg rice bags, and multi-pack cleaning products are consistently cheaper than supermarket apps.
  • Reliability: delivery windows are accurate, and returns are painless. Amazon's logistics network is harder to beat in the Gulf than people assume.
  • Coverage: UAE and Saudi Arabia, with better coverage in smaller cities than Talabat or Noon Daily.

Where it falls short

  • No fresh produce: Amazon sells some packaged fruit and veg, but it's not a grocery delivery app for your weekly vegetable shop.
  • Delivery speed: next-day or two-day for most items, not 30-minute on-demand.
  • Minimum spend for free delivery: usually AED 100/SAR 100 unless you have Prime.

Best for: monthly bulk buys of non-perishables. Use Amazon for staples, and a faster app for fresh food.

5. InstaShop — Fastest premium option in the UAE

InstaShop is the "premium" convenience store app. It connects you to nearby supermarkets, pharmacies, and specialty stores with delivery in 15-45 minutes. It's not cheap, but when you need one or two things urgently, it's the fastest option in the UAE.

What stood out

  • Speed: the fastest average delivery in our tests. 20 minutes was typical for small orders from a nearby store.
  • Range: includes Spinneys, Waitrose, pharmacies, pet shops, and even some alcohol retailers (where legally permitted).
  • App experience: clean, fast, and easy to navigate. The best-designed app in this list.
  • Small-order friendly: unlike some apps that punish a AED 30 basket with AED 10 delivery, InstaShop often has reasonable fees on small baskets.

Where it falls short

  • Price: product prices are higher than Carrefour or Lulu. A litre of milk can cost AED 1-2 more through InstaShop than in-store.
  • Limited to UAE: no Saudi coverage.
  • Not for large shops: fees and markups make it expensive for a full weekly grocery run.

Best for: urgent top-ups — you've run out of milk, need medicine, or forgot one ingredient. Also useful for premium products from Spinneys or Waitrose.

Saudi Arabia-specific options

The UAE grocery delivery market is mature, but Saudi Arabia has its own strong players:

  • Jahez: primarily a restaurant delivery app, but expanding into groceries in Riyadh and Jeddah. Fast and reliable for restaurant-style convenience, but grocery range is still limited.
  • HungerStation: similar to Jahez, restaurant-first with some grocery partners. Good for bundling a food order with a few household items.
  • Nana: Saudi-born grocery app focused on hypermarket partners. Strong in Riyadh, Jeddah, Dammam, and Khobar. Often the best Carrefour-like experience in KSA.
  • Merkato: newer Saudi player with aggressive pricing and fast delivery in major cities. Worth checking if you're in Riyadh.

For most Saudi households, Noon Daily or Nana is the best default for a full grocery shop, with Amazon.sa for bulk staples.

The two that look cheap but aren't

1. Small convenience-store apps with "free delivery"

Many neighbourhood stores run their own WhatsApp or simple app delivery. The delivery is often free, but the item prices are inflated 20-50% above supermarket prices. We compared one small app in Dubai Marina: a basket of 10 items cost AED 142, while the same basket from Carrefour via Talabat Mart cost AED 98. The "free delivery" cost AED 44 more.

2. In-store "premium" supermarkets delivered through third-party apps

Ordering from Spinneys or Waitrose through a third-party app often means paying the premium store price plus the app's service fee plus delivery. If you want Spinneys quality, use InstaShop directly; if you want value, use Talabat Mart with a Carrefour partner.

How to save money on grocery delivery

  1. Rotate apps based on the basket. Use Noon Daily for staples, Amazon for bulk, Kibsons for fresh produce, and InstaShop only for emergencies.
  2. Use subscription plans if you order twice a month. Noon One and Talabat Pro pay for themselves quickly.
  3. Compare the same basket across two apps. Prices change weekly. A 2-minute comparison can save AED 30-50 on a weekly shop.
  4. Avoid peak-hour slots. Friday evenings and public holiday weekends bring delays and higher delivery fees.
  5. Build a "subscribe and save" list. Monthly staples like rice, oil, detergent, and diapers should be on Amazon Subscribe & Save, not on-demand apps.

The bottom line

For most households in the UAE, the best setup is a combination: Noon Daily for weekly staples, Kibsons for fresh produce, and InstaShop for urgent top-ups. If you want one app to do everything, Talabat Mart is the most reliable all-rounder.

In Saudi Arabia, Noon Daily is the best general-purpose choice, Amazon.sa is the best for bulk staples, and Nana is the strongest local grocery app in major cities.

Delivery fees and markups add up. The smartest approach is to match the app to the job — not to default to whichever app is already on your phone.
