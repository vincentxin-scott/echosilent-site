Moving to Dubai 2026: The Complete Relocation Checklist

Everything you need before, during, and after moving to Dubai in 2026 — visas, housing, banking, driving, healthcare, schools and utilities.

S
SilentEcho Editors
· July 21, 2026

Dubai is one of the easiest cities in the world to move to on paper — most nationalities get a visa-on-arrival for tourism, employers sponsor work visas quickly, and the government portal ICP and DubaiNow have digitised almost every bureaucratic step. The difficulty is knowing which order to do things in. Miss a step in the first 30 days and you'll waste weeks correcting it.

This checklist covers the full relocation timeline for a typical working expat in 2026 — from the moment you accept the job offer to your third month living here. It's the order I would do it in if I moved to Dubai today.

Phase 1: Before you fly (weeks -8 to -1)

Get your visa lined up first

In 2026, three visa types cover 90% of expats:

  • Employment visa — your employer sponsors this. You sign the offer letter, send them a passport copy, and they issue an entry permit within 5-10 working days. This is the default.
  • Golden visa — 10-year residency for skilled professionals, investors, and exceptional talents. If your salary is AED 30,000+/month in a specialised field, ask your HR to consider this. It removes visa renewal stress for a decade.
  • Remote work visa — 1-year visa for people working remotely for non-UAE employers. You need a salary of USD 3,500+/month. It's rare but worth checking if you're keeping your current job and just relocating.

Most people land on a tourist visa and convert to an employment visa in-country. This works, but it adds 2-3 weeks of waiting. If your employer can sponsor the visa before you fly, ask them to.

Original documents you'll need

Apostille your degree and marriage certificate before you fly. Attestation in Dubai is possible but takes 3-4 weeks and costs more. You'll need:

  • Passport valid for at least 6 months
  • Original degree certificate (attested by your home country's MOFA + UAE Embassy)
  • Marriage certificate if bringing a spouse (attested)
  • Birth certificates for children (attested)
  • Driver's licence (will save you weeks when applying for a UAE licence — see UAE Driving Guide 2026)
  • 6 months of bank statements from your home country (some landlords ask)

Book temporary accommodation

Don't sign a long-term lease sight-unseen. Book 2-4 weeks of temporary accommodation — a hotel apartment or Airbnb — while you look for a permanent place. Hotel apartments in Dubai Marina, JBR, and Business Bay cost AED 300-600/night; budget options in Deira or Al Barsha run AED 150-300/night.

Phase 2: First week (days 1-7)

Day 1-2: Get a local SIM

You'll need a UAE number for everything from food delivery to bank verification. The two carriers are Etisalat and du.

  • Etisalat has wider coverage, slightly higher prices. A basic 5 GB data plan runs ~AED 99/month.
  • du is cheaper and good enough for most areas. Their 5 GB plan is ~AED 85/month.

You can get a tourist SIM at the airport with just your passport. Upgrade to a resident plan once you have your Emirates ID. Read our breakdown of best VPNs for UAE before you land — some services you'll want for banking, video calls, or streaming require a VPN.

Day 3-4: Open a bank account

This is the most important step in your first week. Without a UAE bank account, you can't receive your salary, can't pay DEWA, can't get a credit card. We wrote a full guide: How to Open a UAE Bank Account in 2026.

The short version: Emirates NBD and FAB are the easiest for new arrivals. Bring your passport, visa copy, and entry stamp. Most banks will issue a debit card same-day and a chequebook within a week.

Day 5-7: Medical and Emirates ID

Every new resident must:

  1. Medical fitness test — at an ICP-approved medical centre. Blood test (HIV, Hepatitis B) + chest X-ray. Costs AED 250-350. Results in 24-48 hours.
  2. Emirates ID biometrics — after your medical passes, visit an ICP Customer Happiness Centre for fingerprints and a photo. Your Emirates ID is your single most important document in the UAE. Card production takes 7-14 days.

Until your Emirates ID arrives, you'll use your passport for everything. Some services (bank account upgrades, DEWA activation) require the actual card.

Phase 3: Settle in (weeks 2-4)

Sign a long-term lease

Dubai rentals operate on an annual lease with a one-time security deposit (5% of annual rent) plus agency fee (5% of annual rent). Most landlords require 1-4 post-dated cheques for the year's rent — this is why your bank account needs to be set up early.

In 2026, a typical 1-bedroom runs:

  • Dubai Marina / JBR: AED 75,000-95,000/year
  • Downtown Dubai / DIFC: AED 85,000-120,000/year
  • Business Bay: AED 65,000-90,000/year
  • Al Barsha / JVC: AED 50,000-70,000/year
  • Deira / Bur Dubai: AED 40,000-60,000/year

The rent is negotiable — asking price is rarely the final price. Expect 5-10% off if you offer fewer cheques (1 or 2 cheques beats 4).

Register DEWA (electricity and water)

DEWA is Dubai's utility provider. You'll need:

  • Emirates ID
  • Tenancy contract (Ejari-registered)
  • AED 2,000 deposit (refundable on exit)
  • AED 130 connection fee

Activation takes 24-48 hours. Your landlord or agent usually handles Ejari registration; you handle DEWA.

Set up internet

Two providers, same as mobile:

  • Etisalat eLife — fibre, 250 Mbps to 1 Gbps, AED 299-599/month
  • du Home — fibre, 250 Mbps to 800 Mbps, AED 269-499/month

Both require Emirates ID and a 12-month commitment (early exit fee: ~AED 1,000). Installation takes 3-7 days.

Get your driving licence (or an IDP)

If you plan to drive, read UAE Driving Guide 2026 — the full process is in there. The short version:

  • Tourists can drive on an International Driving Permit (IDP) + home country licence for the duration of their visa.
  • Residents must convert to a UAE licence within the visa validity. If your home country has a licence-swap agreement with UAE (US, UK, Canada, Australia, most EU countries), you can swap directly. Otherwise you'll take classes and a road test.

Health insurance

Health insurance is mandatory in Dubai. Your employer is required by law to provide it. If your employer's plan is basic, consider a top-up plan — a mid-tier plan with dental and optical costs AED 3,000-6,000/year.

Phase 4: Get comfortable (months 2-3)

Schools (if you have children)

Dubai school fees are the second-largest expat expense after housing. For 2026, expect:

  • British curriculum: AED 30,000-90,000/year
  • American curriculum: AED 35,000-95,000/year
  • IB: AED 40,000-100,000/year
  • Indian curriculum (CBSE): AED 8,000-25,000/year

Schools have waiting lists. Apply for the academic year starting September as soon as your visa is confirmed — January-February is not too early.

Car

If you plan to drive, the choice is:

  • Buy: new compact sedans start at AED 60,000 (Toyota Corolla, Honda Civic). SUVs from AED 100,000.
  • Lease: long-term leases from AED 1,500-3,000/month including insurance and maintenance.
  • Rent: monthly rentals from AED 1,200-2,500/month.

For most expats staying 2-3 years, leasing beats buying — you avoid the 30-40% first-year depreciation.

Cost of living baseline

For a single professional living comfortably in Dubai in 2026:

Expense Monthly (AED)
Rent (1BR, mid-area) 6,000
Utilities + internet 600
Groceries 1,500
Dining out (2-3x/week) 1,200
Transport (Metro + Careem) 600
Health insurance top-up 300
Total ~AED 10,200

For a family of four in a 2-bedroom with a car and school fees, double the housing and add AED 4,000-7,000/month for school. Total: AED 22,000-30,000/month. We compared Dubai to Riyadh in detail: Cost of Living Dubai vs Riyadh 2026.

Common pitfalls to avoid

After helping a dozen friends relocate, these are the mistakes that waste the most time:

  1. Signing a lease before you have a UAE bank account. Landlords want post-dated cheques. No cheques, no lease. Open the bank account first.
  2. Not attesting documents before flying. Attestation in Dubai is possible but slow and expensive. Do it at home.
  3. Buying a car in month one. Traffic is real, parking is expensive, and Salik (toll) adds up. Live 2-3 months first to learn the roads and see if you actually need a car. Many expats get by with Metro + Careem.
  4. Skipping the medical insurance upgrade. Employer-provided basic plans often exclude dental, optical, and mental health. A top-up plan is cheap compared to out-of-pocket costs.
  5. Underestimating summer. From May to September, outdoor time is 6 am to 9 am and 6 pm onward. Plan your daily routine around this, or you'll be miserable.

30-60-90 day milestones

A realistic timeline for feeling settled:

  • Day 30: Bank account active, Emirates ID in hand, lease signed, DEWA and internet connected. You can receive salary, pay bills, and live day-to-day.
  • Day 60: Driving licence sorted (or transport routine locked in), health insurance upgraded, local friend group starting to form. You've eaten at your neighbourhood's best restaurants.
  • Day 90: You know which Metro station has the shortest walk to your office, you've found your grocery store of choice, and Dubai feels like home rather than a hotel.

Most expats report that month three is when the city clicks — the bureaucracy is behind you, and you start noticing what makes the city livable rather than just new.

The bottom line

Moving to Dubai in 2026 is bureaucratically easy if you follow the right order: visa first, SIM second, bank account third, Emirates ID fourth, lease fifth. Everything else — internet, driving licence, schools — flows from those five steps. Skip a step and you'll wait weeks. Follow the order and you're fully set up in 30 days.

The hard part isn't the paperwork — it's the social and practical adjustment. Make friends through your building, your gym, or a hobby group in the first 90 days. Dubai has no "local" population in the traditional sense; everyone is from somewhere else. That makes it one of the easiest cities in the world to land in — but only if you take the first step to connect.
