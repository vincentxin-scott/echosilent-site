Best Gaming Phones 2026: Mobile Esports Power for UAE and Saudi Arabia

2026's best gaming phones — tested for heat, battery, latency, and FPS stability in the Gulf's mobile-first gaming market.

S
SilentEcho Editors
· July 29, 2026

Mobile gaming is not a secondary platform in the Gulf — for many players, it's the only platform. PUBG Mobile, Free Fire, Call of Duty Mobile, and Honor of Kings dominate. Saudi Arabia is investing heavily in esports, and Dubai hosts regional mobile esports tournaments year-round. If you take mobile gaming seriously, your phone's processor, cooling, and touch response matter more than its camera.

After testing seven 2026 gaming and flagship phones across Riyadh and Dubai — indoors, outdoors, and in long-session competitive play — four stood out. Here's what worked, what didn't, and the one phone we couldn't recommend despite the gaming brand.

How we tested

We measured three things:

  • Frame-rate stability: 60-minute sessions of PUBG Mobile (Smooth/Extreme), Genshin Impact (highest settings), and Call of Duty Mobile. We logged fps with GameBench.
  • Skin temperature and throttling: IR thermometer on the back of the phone every 10 minutes, plus CPU throttling detection.
  • Touch latency and display response: touchscreen response time and display refresh rate consistency.

All tests were done on Wi-Fi and 5G in climate-controlled rooms (24-26°C), which is the realistic Gulf indoor environment. We also ran a 30-minute outdoor test at 38°C in Dubai to see how summer heat affected performance.

The four picks

1. ASUS ROG Phone 8 Pro — Best overall gaming phone

The ROG Phone 8 Pro is the most focused gaming device on the market in 2026. ASUS has refined the design over four generations, and it shows: the phone stays cool, the battery lasts, and the air triggers give you physical shoulder buttons that actually work.

What stood out

  • Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 with a vapour chamber: 60-minute PUBG Mobile sessions averaged 89.7 fps on the 90 Hz mode with no visible throttling. After 90 minutes, the back reached 39°C — warm, not hot.
  • AirTriggers and AeroActive Cooler X: the ultrasonic shoulder buttons are genuinely useful in shooters. The optional clip-on cooler drops temperatures by another 4-6°C during marathon sessions.
  • 6,500 mAh battery: the largest in this list. Two-hour gaming sessions dropped the battery by 28%. You can realistically game for 6-7 hours without a charger.
  • 165 Hz AMOLED display: the highest refresh rate on any mainstream phone. Most games don't use it, but system navigation and supported titles feel incredibly smooth.

Where it falls short

  • Camera: competent but not flagship-level. The main sensor is fine; the ultrawide and telephoto are average.
  • Size and weight: 227 g and 8.9 mm thick. It's a pocket anchor.
  • Price: AED 4,500-5,000. You pay for the specialisation.
  • Software updates: ASUS promises two OS updates and four years of security. Samsung and Apple offer longer.

Best for: serious mobile esports players who want every hardware advantage. If you play competitively or stream from your phone, this is the device.

Price in UAE: ~AED 4,700. Saudi Arabia: ~SAR 4,800.

2. RedMagic 9S Pro — Best budget gaming phone

The RedMagic 9S Pro delivers 85% of the ROG Phone experience at half the price. It's the gaming phone for people who want the hardware but don't care about the brand tax.

What stood out

  • Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 + active cooling fan: a tiny internal fan keeps the phone cooler than any passive-cooled flagship. After 60 minutes of Genshin Impact, it was 3-4°C cooler than the Galaxy S24 Ultra in the same test.
  • 120 Hz AMOLED display with 960 Hz touch sampling: touch response feels instant. In fast-paced shooters, the difference between 240 Hz and 960 Hz touch sampling is noticeable.
  • 6,000 mAh battery with 80W charging: charges from 0-100% in about 35 minutes.
  • Shoulder triggers: capacitive touch triggers, not as precise as ASUS's AirTriggers, but good enough for casual competitive play.

Where it falls short

  • Software: RedMagic OS is heavy, occasionally buggy, and slow with updates. The UI looks dated next to Samsung's One UI or Apple's iOS.
  • Camera: below average. Photos come out soft and oversaturated in low light.
  • Availability: officially sold in the UAE and Saudi Arabia, but warranty support is weaker than ASUS or Samsung.

Best for: budget-conscious gamers who want maximum gaming performance for minimum spend. The best price-to-performance gaming phone in 2026.

Price in UAE: ~AED 2,400. Saudi Arabia: ~SAR 2,450.

3. iPhone 16 Pro Max — Best for iOS gaming and esports

Most mobile esports tournaments run on iPhones because iOS delivers the most consistent frame times and the lowest touch latency. The iPhone 16 Pro Max continues that tradition with the A18 Pro chip and a larger vapour chamber than the previous generation.

What stood out

  • A18 Pro chip: in real-world gaming, it's roughly equivalent to the Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 but with better sustained performance. Frame-rate graphs are flatter — fewer micro-stutters.
  • iOS ecosystem: if your friends, clan, or tournament scene is on iOS, this is the phone to buy. Cross-platform games work fine, but social and competitive circles often cluster on iOS.
  • Battery life: despite a smaller battery than the ROG Phone, iOS efficiency means 60-minute gaming sessions only drop the battery by 22%.
  • Display: 120 Hz ProMotion with excellent colour accuracy. iPhones also have the lowest touch latency in the industry.

Where it falls short

  • Price: AED 5,500+ for the 256 GB model. The most expensive phone on this list.
  • No cooling accessories: Apple doesn't sell a clip-on cooler, and third-party options are limited compared to Android.
  • No shoulder buttons: you rely entirely on on-screen controls or third-party controllers.
  • Heat in summer: the outdoor 38°C test caused thermal throttling after 20 minutes of Genshin Impact. In AC, it's fine. Outside, it's not.

Best for: competitive players in the iOS ecosystem, or anyone who wants the smoothest, most consistent gaming experience without specialised hardware.

Price in UAE: ~AED 5,500. Saudi Arabia: ~SAR 5,600.

4. Samsung Galaxy S24 Ultra — Best all-rounder that games

The Galaxy S24 Ultra isn't a gaming phone, but it's the best phone that also happens to be excellent for gaming. If you want one device for work, camera, and PUBG sessions, this is it.

What stood out

  • Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 for Galaxy: a slightly overclocked version of the standard chip. Gaming performance is within 5-10% of the ROG Phone 8 Pro for the first 30 minutes.
  • S Pen and productivity: the only gaming-capable phone with a built-in stylus. Useful for note-taking, signing documents, and some games.
  • Display: 6.8-inch 120 Hz AMOLED with 2600 nits peak brightness. The brightest screen in this list — visible even in direct sunlight.
  • Camera system: the best camera on this list by a wide margin. If you want one phone for everything, this is it.

Where it falls short

  • Thermal throttling: after 45 minutes of sustained gaming, performance drops to roughly 80% of peak. Not a problem for casual sessions, noticeable in ranked play.
  • No gaming-specific hardware: no shoulder buttons, no active cooling, no 165 Hz display.
  • Price: AED 5,100. Only slightly cheaper than the ROG Phone 8 Pro, but you're paying for the camera and screen, not gaming features.

Best for: people who game seriously but not professionally, and who need a flagship phone for work and photography.

Price in UAE: ~AED 5,100. Saudi Arabia: ~SAR 5,200.

The one we don't recommend: Nubia Z60 Ultra

Nubia makes technically capable phones, and the Z60 Ultra has a Snapdragon 8 Gen 3, a huge battery, and a reasonable price. But:

  • The under-display camera is distracting: a faint pixel grid appears over the camera area in bright scenes. Fine for calls, annoying for gaming.
  • Software support is weak: expect one OS update and slow security patches.
  • Availability and warranty: harder to service in the UAE and Saudi Arabia than ASUS, Samsung, or Apple.
  • No gaming-specific features: at AED 3,000, it sits between the RedMagic and the flagships without beating either at their own game.

Skip unless you specifically want the under-display camera and full-screen design.

What matters most in the Gulf

Heat management

Summer temperatures in the Gulf regularly exceed 45°C. Even indoors, if your AC can't keep the room below 28°C, your phone will throttle. This is why dedicated gaming phones with vapour chambers or active fans (ROG, RedMagic) outperform regular flagships in long sessions. If you game outside — in a car, on a terrace, at an outdoor event — expect thermal throttling within 20-30 minutes on any phone.

Practical tip: buy a phone cooler (AED 100-250) if you play for more than an hour at a time. It extends peak performance by 30-60 minutes and protects battery health.

5G and latency

Mobile esports in the Gulf depends on low ping. Both Etisalat and du in the UAE, and STC and Mobily in Saudi Arabia, have strong 5G networks in major cities. Ping to European servers is typically 80-120 ms; ping to Middle East servers (Dubai, Riyadh, Bahrain) is 20-40 ms. A gaming phone won't fix bad routing, but a stable 5G connection and a good mesh Wi-Fi setup at home will.

Screen brightness

If you play near windows or outside, display brightness matters. The Galaxy S24 Ultra's 2600 nits and the iPhone 16 Pro Max's 2000 nits are usable in partial sunlight. The ROG Phone 8 Pro and RedMagic 9S Pro are dimmer — fine indoors, hard to see outside.

How to choose

Use this decision tree:

  1. Competitive Android player: ASUS ROG Phone 8 Pro.
  2. Budget gamer: RedMagic 9S Pro.
  3. iOS ecosystem or tournament player: iPhone 16 Pro Max.
  4. One phone for work, camera, and gaming: Samsung Galaxy S24 Ultra.
  5. Casual gamer who plays 30 minutes a day: any 2026 flagship with Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 or A18 Pro will do.

The bottom line

For most serious mobile gamers in the Gulf, the ASUS ROG Phone 8 Pro is the right answer. It has the cooling, battery, shoulder buttons, and display to match the region's competitive mobile gaming scene. If you can't justify the price, the RedMagic 9S Pro delivers most of the performance for half the money.

If you're already in the Apple ecosystem or play in iOS tournaments, the iPhone 16 Pro Max is the safest choice. And if you want one phone for everything — work calls, weekend photography, and ranked PUBG matches — the Samsung Galaxy S24 Ultra is the only flagship that doesn't force you to compromise.

Whatever you pick, remember: in the Gulf, heat is the enemy. The best gaming phone is the one that stays fast after 60 minutes in an air-conditioned room.
